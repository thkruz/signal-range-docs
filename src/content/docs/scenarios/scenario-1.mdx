### Mission Briefing

You are a Ground Station Operator at North Atlantic Teleport Services, a commercial satellite ground station facility located in rural Vermont. Your company provides ground segment services for multiple GEO communication satellites serving the North Atlantic region.

Your latest client, SeaLink Communications, launched MARINER-1 fourteen days ago aboard a Falcon 9 from Cape Canaveral. The satellite completed its apogee burns over the past two weeks and reached its operational slot at 53°W geostationary orbit yesterday. Beacon Orbital Analytics in Cambridge provided orbital analysis throughout the transfer orbit phase and confirmed the satellite achieved station-keeping this morning.

The spacecraft operations team at SeaLink's control center in Halifax has completed initial checkout - solar arrays deployed, batteries charged, attitude control stable. They've now handed the communications payload over to ground operations. You are conducting the first ground station RF link test.

This is a critical milestone. Until this link is established and verified, MARINER-1 cannot begin revenue service. The satellite is designed to provide C-band maritime connectivity from Newfoundland to the Caribbean - fishing fleets, cargo vessels, cruise ships, offshore platforms.

**Who's Watching:**
- **Catherine Vega** (SeaLink CEO): Former submarine communications officer. Bet the company on this satellite. Has already drafted two versions of tomorrow's press release - one congratulatory, one apologetic.
- **Empire Insurance Syndicate Observer**: Represented via video link from New York City. Known for their no-nonsense approach and colorful language. They have a reputation for being thorough to the point of paranoia, and their motto is "If it can go wrong, it will."
- **Captain James Okafor** (Atlantic Shipping Alliance): Your anchor customer. Runs 47 container ships. Tired of paying Inmarsat rates. Will either sign a 10-year contract tomorrow or walk away.
- **Francis Martin**: Board Member and former investment banker who doesn't understand satellites but understands burn rate. Keeps asking "is it working yet?" on Slack.

This test either succeeds, or very expensive conversations begin.

### Detailed Step-by-Step Procedure

1.  **Phase 1: Power-Up Sequence (5 minutes)**
    1.  GPS Disciplined Oscillator (GPSDO)
        *   Verify GPSDO unit is powered on
        *   Verify GPSDO unit is warm (the OCXO will alarm if not at operating temp)
        *   Verify GPS antenna has satellite lock (green LED)
        *   Wait for frequency lock (30-60 seconds)
        *   Success: "LOCKED" status, \<5×10⁻¹¹ stability
        *   **Critical:** ALL other equipment depends on this reference!
    2.  Low Noise Block (LNB)
        *   Power on LNB (converts 3.7-4.2 GHz down to 950-1450 MHz IF)
        *   Set gain: 55 dB
        *   Wait for temperature stabilization: 2-3 minutes
        *   Success: temperature stable and no frequency drift
    3.  Block Upconverter (BUC)
        *   **DO NOT TRANSMIT YET** - antenna must be pointed first
        *   Power on BUC (standby mode)
        *   Set to C-band mode (5.925-6.425 GHz)
        *   Keep RF output MUTED
        *   Success: Ready state, but no RF output
    4.  Spectrum Analyzer
        *   Power on unit
        *   Select RF mode (direct C-band input)
        *   Set center frequency: 3,702.5 MHz
        *   Set span: 10 MHz
        *   Set RBW: 10 kHz
        *   Set reference level: -40 dBm
        *   Success: Clean baseline noise floor visible
2.  **Phase 2: Antenna Pointing & Beacon Acquisition (8-10 minutes)**
    1.  Antenna Control Unit (ACU)
        *   Enter satellite parameters: Name: MARINER-1, Longitude: 53.0°W, Latitude: 0.0° (GEO)
        *   Calculated look angles: Azimuth: 161.8° ± 0.5°, Elevation: 34.2° ± 0.5°
    2.  Initial Pointing
        *   Command antenna to calculated position
        *   Watch for: Antenna begins moving (15-20 seconds transit time for 11m dish)
        *   Verify: Azimuth and elevation encoders show correct angles
        *   Wait for "ON TARGET" indication
    3.  Beacon Search
        *   Look at spectrum analyzer: You should see a carrier!
        *   Expected beacon: Strong CW carrier at 3,702.5 MHz
        *   Typical level: -70 to -80 dBm (53°W is moderate elevation from Vermont)
        *   If beacon is weak or offset:
            *   Use antenna fine-pointing (±0.2° adjustments)
            *   Peak the signal by maximizing amplitude
            *   Goal: Achieve at least -75 dBm beacon level
    4.  Lock Status Verification
        *   Beacon should be stable (not drifting)
        *   Frequency should be within ±1 kHz of 3,702.5 MHz
        *   Carrier-to-Noise (C/N): Should show >15 dB
        *   Log beacon parameters for operations report
3.  **Phase 3: Transmit Path Checkout (5 minutes)**
    1.  Transmitter Configuration
        *   Power on Transmitter Modem #1
        *   Configure modem: Modulation: QPSK, Symbol Rate: 5.0 Msps, FEC: 3/4, Scrambling: ON
        *   Set IF output: 140.0 MHz (will be upconverted to 5,925.0 MHz)
    2.  BUC Configuration for Transmit
        *   Set uplink frequency: 5,925.0 MHz
        *   Set transmit power: -10 dBm
        *   Double-check antenna pointing
        *   Coordinate with satellite ops: "NATV-1 ready to transmit on Transponder 6A"
        *   **CRITICAL:** Get explicit permission before unmuting
    3.  Low-Power RF Test
        *   Unmute BUC RF output
        *   Verify on spectrum analyzer: Clean QPSK signal at correct frequency
        *   Listen for satellite ops confirmation: "NATV-1, we see your carrier on Transponder 6A"
        *   If no confirmation: MUTE immediately, troubleshoot
4.  **Phase 4: Receive Path Verification (5 minutes)**
    1.  Downlink Monitoring
        *   Change spectrum analyzer to monitor downlink: Center frequency: 3,700.0 MHz, Span: 50 MHz
        *   You should see: Your uplink signal transponded back
        *   Expected level: -60 to -70 dBm
        *   Verify: Signal is in correct location in transponder
    2.  Receiver Configuration
        *   Power on Receiver Modem #1
        *   Set to monitor Transponder 6A downlink
        *   Configure demodulator: Modulation: QPSK, Symbol Rate: 5.0 Msps, FEC: 3/4, Carrier frequency: 3,700.0 MHz
        *   Wait for modem lock: 5-10 seconds
        *   Success indicators: "CARRIER LOCK" LED, "FRAME LOCK" LED, Eb/No: >8 dB
    3.  Loopback Test
        *   Transmit a test pattern from modem TX port
        *   Verify receipt at modem RX port

        *   This confirms: Full duplex link is operational!
5.  **Phase 5: Power Ramp & Link Optimization (5 minutes)**
    1.  Gradual Power Increase
        *   Increase BUC power in 3 dB steps: -10 dBm → -7 dBm → -4 dBm → -1 dBm → +2 dBm
        *   Monitor at each step: Satellite ops confirms no saturation, Downlink power increases, No adjacent channel interference
        *   Target transmit power: +2 dBm (28.5 dBm EIRP)
    2.  Link Margin Verification
        *   Record final link parameters: Uplink C/N: \_\_\_\_\_ dB, Downlink C/N: \_\_\_\_\_ dB, Combined Eb/No: \_\_\_\_\_ dB

        *   Notify Satellite Ops: "NATV-1 declares MARINER-1 Transponder 6A link OPERATIONAL"

### Success Criteria

*   ✅ GPSDO locked and providing stable reference
*   ✅ Beacon acquired at >-75 dBm
*   ✅ Antenna pointing optimized (peaking completed)
*   ✅ Transmit carrier visible on satellite (confirmed by sat ops)
*   ✅ Downlink signal received and demodulated
*   ✅ BER < 1×10⁻⁹ on loopback test
*   ✅ Link margin >2 dB at operational power
*   ✅ No interference to adjacent satellites/transponders

### Common New Operator Mistakes to Avoid

*   ❌ Transmitting before antenna is pointed (radiating in wrong direction!)
*   ❌ Forgetting to lock GPSDO first (everything will drift)
*   ❌ Using too much transmit power initially (can damage satellite)
*   ❌ Not monitoring spectrum during transmit (blind operation)
*   ❌ Forgetting to coordinate with satellite operations team
