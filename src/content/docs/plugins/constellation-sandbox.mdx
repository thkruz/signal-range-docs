Range Watch is a sandbox campaign. Its scenarios load real satellite catalogs and let you explore.
There is no timer, nothing can fail and nothing is locked. It is marked alpha. Each scenario has a
short list of objectives that guide you, but you can ignore them.

![GEO Belt Survey scenario at the High Desert station.](/images/shots/plugins/constellation-sandbox.webp)

## The two scenarios

| Scenario | Station | Catalog | Equipment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Starlink Interference** | Cedar Mesa, CM-01 | The Starlink fleet, about 11,200 satellites, plus one GEO satellite, KU-SAT 1 at 98 W | Ku-band phased array user terminal, Ku-band RF front end, spectrum analyzer, RX modem |
| **GEO Belt Survey** | High Desert, HD-01 | The geostationary belt | 3-meter Ku-band manual antenna, Ku-band RF front end, spectrum analyzer, RX modem |

Both start on 9 November 2026 at 18:00:00 UTC.

## Starlink Interference

You run a phased array terminal under the GEO arc and see how it hands over between Starlinks.
See [Phased Array](/plugins/phased-array/) for the page and its controls.

<Steps>

1. **Open Array Control.** Open the tab on CM-01.
2. **Watch Two Handovers.** Leave the terminal in AUTO until it hands over twice. Skip time forward if you like.
3. **Lock a Starlink by Hand.** Use MANUAL and click a satellite low in the sky until the scan loss passes 1 dB.
4. **Test the GEO Protection Zone.** Turn the zone off, lock a satellite whose beam lies in the GEO arc, then turn the zone on again.
5. **Report the Link Drop.** Return to AUTO and let it hand over. Read **Last link drop**, then open the report form. File the NORAD number of the serving satellite and a UTC window of at most 10 minutes. The report is accepted only if the terminal logged a drop by that satellite in that window.

</Steps>

## GEO Belt Survey

You look for occupied slots in the geostationary belt and identify satellites by their beacons.

<Steps>

1. **Open the World Map.** Turn on the GEO belt layer.
2. **Pin a GEO Satellite.** Search the asset tree for a GEO payload above the horizon.
3. **Point the Dish at It.** On ACU Control, in MANUAL, step azimuth and elevation to the values on the pinned satellite page, then press APPLY. The dish moves slowly. The beam is under 1 degree wide and slots are about 1 degree apart.
4. **Read the Carriers.** Open RX Analysis and nudge the dish until modem 1 reads LOCKED with a C/N of at least 10 dB. Only the vertical carriers can lock. A narrow spike is a beacon, not a carrier.
5. **Convert IF to RF.** The LNB local oscillator is at 13100 MHz.
6. **Tune a Beacon.** Find a narrow spike between 11200 and 11700 MHz RF and tune modem 1 to it with a 1 MHz bandwidth.
7. **Confirm Which Satellite It Is.** Use the Beacon ID form. Its lookup turns a NORAD number into a beacon frequency and back. The answer is accepted only if modem 1 is measuring that satellite.

</Steps>

An occupied slot shows a row of carriers on the spectrum analyzer. An empty one shows only noise.
Traffic carriers look the same on every slot because operators reuse frequencies. Beacons differ
because each satellite's beacon frequency is set by its NORAD number.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Modem will not lock on a carrier | Horizontal carriers are rejected by this feed, or the dish is slightly off. | Use a vertical carrier and nudge in 0.01 degree steps. |
| Nothing on the analyzer | The slot is empty, or the dish is between slots. | Re-check the pinned satellite's az and el. |
| Outage report rejected | The window is longer than 10 minutes, or no drop by that satellite is in it. | Read **Last link drop** again and narrow the window. |
| No handover happens | The terminal is in MANUAL or STOW. | Press **AUTO**. |
