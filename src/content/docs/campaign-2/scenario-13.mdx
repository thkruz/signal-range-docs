### The zenith pass

An elevation-over-azimuth pedestal has a keyhole at the zenith. SAR-2 rises from 163° and sets at 345°: as it crosses 89.5° the azimuth the pedestal has to point at swings almost 180° in a few seconds, and the rate it needs goes to infinity through the top. At 20°/s the pedestal cannot, the 0.45° beam falls off the spacecraft near culmination, and the link is gone until the pedestal catches up on the descending leg. The 73° pass in scenario 8 held because a few degrees a second is still tracking; the last few degrees to the zenith are not.

| Leg | Expect |
|-----|--------|
| Ascending, from about 25° | Lock, C/N ≥ 8 dB, rising toward the top |
| Through 13:16:56 | The pedestal loses the bird and the receiver drops lock for several seconds. Watch it on RX analysis; do not touch the pedestal |
| Descending | Lock comes back once the azimuth rate falls. Hold it for 100 s, then record the cause |

This is an operations note, not a fault. Maintenance cannot repair geometry.

## Three Candidates for the SAR-1 Shortfall

| Candidate | Where it shows | How to rule it out |
|-----------|----------------|--------------------|
| Element-set age | Pass Schedule ephemeris panel; a pointing miss program-track cannot hold | Panel shows current elements and no pending update; program-track holds lock through the pass |
| Pointing | The beacon off boresight on RX analysis | The beacon peaks on boresight at culmination |
| Receive chain | RX analysis, LNB panel: noise temperature against the 60 K survey value | It is the only one left. Read it |

<Aside type="caution">
Do not power-cycle the LNB to "fix" it. A cold LNA starts at twice its nominal noise temperature and settles back to where it was: worse, then the same. The LNA is the first stage, so its noise temperature is very nearly the whole system temperature, and 10 log(T_now / T_survey) is the shortfall to the tenth of a decibel. That is a maintenance ticket, with the number attached.
</Aside>
