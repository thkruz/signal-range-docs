### The three warnings

The logger scores rarity; you score meaning. Three entries carry warning severity this month, and each is dispositioned on who, what and when:

| Entry | Actor | What | Disposition |
|-------|-------|------|-------------|
| 06:30 | op-fiona | Console login from a source address the station has never seen | Shetland went operational this week; its address is new to the rule. Expected, explained, checkable against the site register. |
| 03:00 | svc-monitor | Configuration change to its own poll interval, change ticket CHG-0412 | The right actor doing the thing it exists to do, when the ticket said. Off-hours is a property of people, not of a scheduler. |
| 02:47 | op-guest | Six failed logins, off-hours | An account that should no longer exist, at an hour nobody authorised should be trying it. **Finding**, whether or not anything got in. |

<Aside type="caution">
Severity is a hint, not a verdict. An entry earns a flag on what it is, when it happened, and who did it, not on whether it succeeded. Six failed attempts that never got in are still six attempts, at an hour when nobody should have been trying.
</Aside>

**Priya's order:** flag the entry, note the time you saw it, then disable the account. The order matters to her later.

## Escalation

**Handle yourself:**
- Flagging entries for the return, with dispositions
- Disabling accounts whose authorisation has lapsed; expiring accounts whose holders have left
- Signing off the trail

**Ring Charlie, then London:**
- Any successful authentication by an account that should not have one
- Configuration or command entries by an actor you cannot account for
- Evidence that key material was touched outside a scheduled rotation

None of the above is expected today. That is why you read the whole log rather than the interesting-looking bits.

## End of Briefing

"Nothing happened" is a conclusion, not an assumption. You reach it by reading everything, and a return that says so is only worth anything if you did.

A dormant account with maintenance privileges is exposure whether or not anyone used it. If somebody was trying it at three in the morning, London will want to know that too. And a baseline that cost the station a pass would not be a baseline; it would be an outage with paperwork.
