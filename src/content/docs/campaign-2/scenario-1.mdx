<Aside type="caution">
A 4 m dish at Ku has a beam under half a degree wide. Manual pointing cannot follow a moving LEO bird — put the antenna in **program-track** before AOS and let it fly the pass. (The pedestal slews at up to 20°/s, so these passes track cleanly; a high, near-zenith pass is a different animal — one for a later shift.)
</Aside>

## Link Parameters

LNB is high-side injection: **IF = LO − RF** (13,100 MHz LO).

| Signal | RF (MHz) | IF (MHz) | Format |
|--------|----------|----------|--------|
| SAR-1 imagery downlink | 11,686 | 1,414 | QPSK 3/4, 36 MHz |
| SAR-1 telemetry beacon | 11,711 | 1,389 | CW |
| SAR-2 imagery downlink | 11,730 | 1,370 | QPSK 3/4, 36 MHz |
| SAR-2 telemetry beacon | 11,703 | 1,397 | CW |

Expect up to ±270 kHz of Doppler at Ku across a pass. The 36 MHz video carrier shrugs it off; the beacons will walk across the display — the spectrum analyzer and RX analysis views are your confirmation that you're on the right bird.

### Commissioning Acceptance (test card GW-01-FL-001)

| Check | Pass Criterion |
|-------|----------------|
| Downlink C/N, high-elevation segment | ≥ 8 dB, held through the decode |
| Predicted peak C/N at max elevation | ~11 dB (flag to Rotterdam if it comes in more than 3 dB low) |
| Decode window | C/N crosses 8 dB near 18° elevation on the way up and peaks at culmination — roughly max-el ± 1.5 minutes. Do not chase a decode at the horizon. |
