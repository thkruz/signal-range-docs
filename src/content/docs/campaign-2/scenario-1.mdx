The scheduler works the 5° elevation mask, not the geometric horizon. SAR-1 actually breaks the horizon at 14:02 from azimuth 005°; it clears the mask a minute later due north. Read AOS off the **Pass Schedule** tab.

<Aside type="caution">
A 4 m dish at Ku has a beam under half a degree wide. Manual pointing cannot follow a moving LEO bird. Park the antenna on the rise azimuth by hand, then put it in **program-track** and let it fly the pass. The pedestal slews at 20°/s, so a 28° pass tracks cleanly; a near-zenith pass is a different animal and one for a later shift.
</Aside>

## Link Parameters

LNB is high-side injection: **IF = LO − RF** (13,100 MHz LO). Vermont was low-side. Same arithmetic, opposite sign, and the spectrum is inverted: a higher RF lands at a lower IF.

| Signal | RF (MHz) | IF (MHz) | Format |
|--------|----------|----------|--------|
| SAR-1 imagery downlink | 11,686 | 1,414 | QPSK 3/4, 36 MHz |
| SAR-1 telemetry beacon | 11,711 | 1,389 | CW |
| SAR-2 imagery downlink | 11,730 | 1,370 | QPSK 3/4, 36 MHz |
| SAR-2 telemetry beacon | 11,703 | 1,397 | CW |

### Doppler

At 11.7 GHz the range rate on these passes is worth about **±270 kHz**: +274 kHz as SAR-1 breaks the horizon, zero at culmination, −271 kHz at LOS. The 36 MHz imagery carrier shrugs it off. The CW beacon does not: on a 2 MHz span it walks visibly across the analyzer. Because the LO is high-side, the beacon appears **below** 1,389 MHz while the bird approaches and **above** it after culmination.

### Acceptance

| Check | Pass Criterion |
|-------|----------------|
| Downlink C/N, high-elevation segment | ≥ 8 dB, held through the decode |
| C/N at culmination | ≥ 9 dB (predicted ~11 dB; flag to Rotterdam if more than 3 dB low) |
| Decode window | C/N crosses 8 dB near 18° elevation on the way up, peaks at culmination: roughly max-el ± 1.5 min. Do not chase a decode at the horizon. |
