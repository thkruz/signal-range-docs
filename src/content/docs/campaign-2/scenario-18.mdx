## If It Comes Back

Monday's carrier was continuous. Today's is not.

| Read | On the analyzer | What it argues |
|------|-----------------|----------------|
| Position | Inside the band of the bird you are receiving | Whoever runs it knows the plan |
| Doppler | None, while the video walks ~200 kHz | Ground, not space |
| Cadence | 60 s on, 30 s off, to the second | A timer - intent, not accident |
| Shetland | Clean on the same pass | Galway is the target, not the satellite |
| Key status | Valid; frames decode in the off phase | RF denial, not intrusion |

<Aside type="caution">
The pass is not over when the carrier arrives. You receive on the frequency the satellite transmits on; there is nowhere to retune to. The tool is the **IF notch**: centre it on the carrier, 2 MHz wide, 30 dB deep. The video loses a sliver of its 36 MHz and keeps its lock. A receiver you switch off is a pass they took from you for free.
</Aside>

## The Notch Filter

Two notches available on the RX Analysis tab. Each takes a centre (MHz IF), a bandwidth (MHz) and a depth (dB); power the module and apply.

| Notch | Centre | Width | Depth | For |
|-------|--------|-------|-------|-----|
| 0 | 1,374 MHz | 2 MHz | 30 dB | Event 1 - the SAR-2 pass |
| 1 | 1,410 MHz | 2 MHz | 30 dB | Event 2 - the SAR-1 pass |

Notch 0 stays where it is when you retune for SAR-1. If the carrier moves with the plan, it will not be at 1,374.
