## Transfer Rule - M&C Enclave

Nothing enters the M&C segment on removable media except through the transfer station:

1. A media ticket, raised in advance, naming the file, its source and its hash.
2. The file scanned and hash-checked on the isolated kiosk.
3. Moved across the one-way path onto site-owned media.
4. Logged with two names on it.

Personal or vendor media never mounts on an M&C host. The rule is slow on purpose: the question "what is on this stick" is never answered on the machine that moves the dish.

## Firmware - Trusting What Is Installed

A firmware image installs on a controller only when all four hold:

| Requirement | What it proves |
|-------------|----------------|
| Vendor-signed release, published with a hash | The vendor made it |
| Obtained through the transfer path | It arrived the way files arrive here |
| Hash matched on the isolated kiosk | It is the file the vendor published |
| Change ticket naming version, window, rollback | Somebody owns it |

A technician's badge does not sign a file.

<Aside type="caution">
Hold, isolate, keep. Do not wipe the workstation or destroy the media before CSIRT has seen them: they are the only record of what was on them.
</Aside>

## Why the ACU Is Not a Laptop

The M&C workstation is on the segment that controls the antenna. The ACU runs firmware nothing on that segment can inspect after it is installed. A scanner catches what it knows; a modified controller image is not on its list. On operational equipment the control is the path the file took, not a scan at the end of it. A controller running a bad image points a four-metre dish wherever the image says, on the next pass.
