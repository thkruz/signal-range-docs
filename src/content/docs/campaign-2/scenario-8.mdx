## The Other Two Contacts

### 02:54 from Shetland: telemetry, on holdover

A 13° pass at 1,233 km will not decode imagery: expect C/N to peak near 6.6 dB, and hold lock above 5 dB through culmination for the state-of-health frames. That is 20 log(1233 / 581) = 6.5 dB more path loss than the 00:31 pass, plus atmosphere at 13°. A site is underperforming only when its number is worse than its geometry.

The SH-02 GNSS antenna has been unreliable in the wind. If the GPS Timing panel shows the receiver locked with HOLDOVER active, the GNSS input switch up and zero satellites, that is an outage at the site, not a switch left down. The disciplined oscillator coasts on its last correction; a few minutes of holdover is a few microseconds and a few hertz at Ku. Fly the pass on it, leave the switch alone (cycling it cannot conjure a signal), and confirm re-lock when the signal returns. Then put the outage in the handover: the day shift decides whether it was weather or a failing antenna, and cannot decide without the record.

### 04:27 over Galway: steep, after the key roll

Peak C/N near 16.5 dB at 73° and 408 km, the best number of the night. The azimuth rate at the top of a 73° pass is a few degrees a second, inside what the 20°/s pedestal can do; the keyhole is a near-zenith problem, for a different shift. Before AOS, read the payload crypto: the fleet traffic key rolled at 04:15 and the station did not follow. Re-key from the TX chain payload panel (one load, both directions) and confirm TX and RX read Valid, or the imagery will not decrypt.

LNB high-side: **IF = LO − RF** (13,100 MHz). BUC low-side: **RF = IF + LO** (12,600 MHz).

| Signal | IF (MHz) | RF (MHz) | Format |
|--------|----------|----------|--------|
| SAR-1 imagery downlink | 1,414 | 11,686 | QPSK 3/4, 36 MHz |
| SAR-1 telemetry beacon | 1,389 | 11,711 | CW |

| SAR-2 imagery downlink | 1,370 | 11,730 | QPSK 3/4, 36 MHz; both sites retune to it |

Doppler: ±270 kHz on the downlink (receiver follows), ±325 kHz on the uplink (compensated at the TT&C console, required for an ACK). A 40° pass has a higher peak range rate than the afternoon passes; the compensation handles it, provided it is on.
