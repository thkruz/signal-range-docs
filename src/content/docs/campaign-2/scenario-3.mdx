## Uplink Plan

BUC is low-side injection: **RF = IF + LO** (12,600 MHz LO). No spectrum inversion on the transmit side. The receive LNB is high-side and unchanged from acceptance.

| Path | IF (MHz) | LO (MHz) | RF (MHz) | Format |
|------|----------|----------|----------|--------|
| SAR-1 telecommand uplink | **1,405** | 12,600 (BUC) | 14,005 | QPSK 3/4, 36 MHz, −7 dBm modem |
| SAR-1 telemetry beacon (down) | 1,389 | 13,100 (LNB) | 11,711 | CW |
| SAR-1 imagery downlink | 1,414 | 13,100 (LNB) | 11,686 | QPSK 3/4, 36 MHz |

The acceptance crew left transmit modem 1 on their 1,400 MHz test carrier. Five megahertz off, and the spacecraft receiver never hears you.

### Doppler Across the Pass

| Link | Frequency | Peak Doppler | Handling |
|------|-----------|--------------|----------|
| Downlink | 11.7 GHz | ±270 kHz | Receiver AFC follows it |
| Uplink | 14.0 GHz | ±325 kHz | Pre-compensated at the console; the spacecraft cannot chase it |

The range rate at AOS is closing at roughly 7 km/s, passes through zero at culmination, and opens at the same rate on the way out. Uplink compensation is a switch on the TT&C tab; it slews the command carrier against the predicted rate so the spacecraft sees 14,005 MHz throughout. Engage it before the pass.

| GPSDO | Locked, 9 satellites | Disciplined; the BUC LO is locked to it |

<Aside type="danger">
Order of operations on this chain is a safety interlock you enforce by hand, one step at a time:

1. Transmit modem on air, into the **muted** BUC.
2. BUC unmuted, with the carrier behind it.
3. HPA output enabled, with the BUC driving it.

Coming down, it mirrors: HPA off, BUC muted, carrier off. Enabling the HPA with nothing driving the BUC amplifies the BUC's own noise floor into the feed and fails the shift.
</Aside>
