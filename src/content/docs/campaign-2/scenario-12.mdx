## SAR-3 Link Parameters

LNB high-side: **IF = LO − RF** (13,100 MHz). BUC low-side: **RF = IF + LO** (12,600 MHz). The two sides of this rack do their arithmetic with opposite signs, and the same 60 MHz mistake is possible on both.

| Signal | IF (MHz) | RF (MHz) | Format |
|--------|----------|----------|--------|
| SAR-3 telemetry beacon | 1,315 | 11,785 | CW |
| SAR-3 imagery downlink (test pattern) | 1,340 | 11,760 | QPSK 3/4, 36 MHz; band 1,322 to 1,358 |
| SAR-3 TT&C uplink | **1,465** | 14,065 | QPSK 3/4, 36 MHz, −7 dBm modem. TX modem 1 is on 1,405 (SAR-1) and must be retuned |
| SAR-3 transponded return | 1,290 | 11,810 | The station's own uplink, turned around; just off the left edge of a 1,330-centred screen |
| SAR-1 imagery downlink (for contrast) | 1,414 | 11,686 | What the RX modem is on now |

Doppler: up to ±270 kHz on the downlink, up to ±325 kHz on the uplink; compensation at the TT&C console is required for an ACK.

### Keys

Two keys, two directions. PLD-ON and the pattern command go up under the **command key**; the test pattern comes down under the **traffic key**. A pattern that will not decrypt looks exactly like a payload that did not power on, and the card cannot tell them apart afterwards. Both read Valid on the card before AOS, so the decode can only mean one thing.

<Aside type="caution">
Secure the uplink before you decode. SAR-3 turns the station's own 14,065 MHz command carrier around through its command transponder at 11,810 MHz, co-polar with the receive chain and inside its band. With the amplifier up that return arrives some 50 dB above the test pattern, the AGC pulls the whole chain down and the video reads about 3 dB of C/N instead of eleven. Nothing on the RX chain is broken; it is doing what an AGC does. Both ACKs are in hand by then; the amplifier has nothing left to do until the next command.
</Aside>

## Transmit Chain Order

| Up (inside the window, after the beacon is in) | Down (after the decode) |
|------------------------------------------------|-------------------------|
| 1. TX modem on air, into the **muted** BUC | 1. HPA output off (already, before the decode) |
| 2. BUC unmuted | 2. BUC muted |
| 3. HPA output enabled; confirm linear | 3. Carrier off |

Every stage sees a signal before it sees gain. An HPA enabled with no drive amplifies the noise floor into the feed; a BUC unmuted with the modem off does the same at lower level. HPA_NOISE_AMPLIFICATION is the invariant this order protects.
