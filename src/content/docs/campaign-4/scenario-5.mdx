## Ranging

A ranging tone goes up the command path like a command - key, window, uplink - and comes back with a number: the slant range from the aperture to the bird, to a tenth of a kilometre. One range is one number on one line of sight. A series of them over time, from a known slot, with the pointing angles the tracker already holds, is an **arc** - and an arc is what an orbit is fitted to.

| Reading | What it tells the engineers |
|---------|------------------------------|
| One range | Almost nothing on its own |
| Three timed ranges | A radial track: where the bird is along the line of sight and how that is changing |
| Three before and three after a known burn | The burn itself, measured from the ground: the step between the two halves |
| The pointing angles held throughout | The other two dimensions the range cannot see |

## The Burn

At 0214Z TALON-2 fires its thrusters and moves a tenth of a degree east along its orbit. From SANDSTORM that is about **1.5 km of slant range** - the fourth tone comes back longer than the third - with the monitor still on the bird and the telemetry still LIVE.

<Aside type="caution">
Range that steps while pointing and telemetry hold steady is the spacecraft moving, not the station. It is not a tracking error, not a tone fault, and not noise. It also means the element set the station is predicting from now describes where the bird used to be: the Pass Schedule tab reads **EPHEMERIS STALE** until LANTERN's post-burn set is loaded from the inbox.
</Aside>
