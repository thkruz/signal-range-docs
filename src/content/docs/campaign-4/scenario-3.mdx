## Reading a Spacecraft

Telemetry is grouped by subsystem. Each channel has a nominal value, a yellow band and a red band. A limit is a distance to harm, not a fraction of scale.

| Subsystem | Channels | What they tell you |
|-----------|----------|--------------------|
| EPS (power) | Bus voltage, battery state of charge, array current | Whether the regulator holds the bus, where the battery sits, whether the sun is on the panels. The number to watch is the battery at the bottom of the next eclipse |
| TCS (thermal) | Battery temperature, TWTA baseplate | The battery has tight limits because a cell above 32 °C ages fast and above 40 is a fire risk; the TWTA is designed to run hot. Slow channels with tight limits are the ones you write down |
| ADCS (attitude) | Reaction wheel speed, sun sensor error | Wheel speed is stored momentum - how hard the bird works to hold still; its trend across contacts says when a momentum dump is due. Sun error says the pointing is good |
| COMM | Transponder output power, command receiver AGC | The payload is alive and hearing you |
| OBC | Processor load | The computer has headroom |

<Aside type="caution">
**Nominal** is a claim about fresh frames inside limits, made after reading them. **No red badges** is not a state of health. The link badge must read LIVE and the frame counter must be climbing before any channel means anything; a STALE stream is frozen history.
</Aside>
