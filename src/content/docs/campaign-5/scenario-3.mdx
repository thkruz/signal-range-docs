## What a Fix Is

COMPUTE FIX returns a centre and a 95% error ellipse. The emitter is inside that ellipse with 95% probability and **nowhere in particular inside it**. The centre is the most likely point, not the point. A field team is searching an area, and the size of the area is the size of the ellipse.

What a fix is not:
- **A point.** Handing a team the centre coordinates and no ellipse sends them to one spot in a region that might be twenty kilometres across.
- **Permanent.** A fix is a statement about where the emitter was during the captures it was solved from. An emitter on wheels makes it history the moment it moves.
- **Self-updating.** If the emitter retunes, the correlator is still tuned to the old carrier and the old captures are still in the solver. The console does not know the emitter moved. You do, if you are watching.

### When the carrier stops

Two full cycles of silence from a carrier that has kept a cadence all night is not a long off-window. Treat it as the emitter having stopped, moved, or retuned:

1. Tell the team to hold short. Do not send them into an ellipse the emitter may have left.
2. Log the time the carrier was last seen. The first fix becomes a last-known position with that time on it.
3. Put max-hold across the whole passband. A retune is the cheapest thing an emitter can do; the new carrier will be somewhere in the 40 MHz.
4. When a new carrier appears on the same cadence: **CLEAR** the correlator, retune it, and capture the new carrier on its own.

<Aside type="caution">
The solver uses every capture on the pair. Six captures from one site and six from another do not average to either; they solve to a place that is neither, with a residual that says so. CLEAR before you capture a carrier from a new position.
</Aside>

## End of Briefing

Somebody has learned that a fix takes a few minutes and a drive takes an hour. They key up, they let you fix them, and they are gone before the truck arrives. The only answer to that is to keep the analyzer on the passband and the team on a short leash, and to treat every fix as the emitter's history rather than its address.
