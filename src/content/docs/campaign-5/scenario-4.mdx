## How to Prove a Fix

A fix that is challenged is defended with the measurement chain, in this order:

1. **Calibrate the instrument.** Fix a known emitter on the same pair, the same night. The Cannon terminal is at 34.383° N, 103.322° W. If its fix lands inside 20 km of that, the pair's geometry, timing, and frequency references are sound. This proves nothing about the hostile. It proves you can trust what the correlator says about the hostile.
2. **Solve the target alone.** CLEAR the console. The solver uses every capture on the pair, and reference captures mixed into the hostile set would drag the hostile fix toward Cannon - and hand the J6 his argument. Retune to the hostile and capture only inside its on-windows.
3. **Close the ellipse past the alternative.** The 95% ellipse is the claim. Keep capturing through further cycles until its semi-major axis is 20 km or less. If the alternative site is farther from the fix centre than the ellipse reaches, the alternative is excluded - not by opinion, by geometry.
4. **State the confidence.** Region, probability, what was excluded, and the state of the emitter when you wrote it.

<Aside type="note">
A reference emitter at a surveyed position is standard practice for any geolocation system. Operators fix it routinely to check the pair; the night somebody challenges a result is the night that habit pays.
</Aside>
