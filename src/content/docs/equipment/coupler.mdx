The coupler is a passive device that samples a small part of the signal at a chosen point in the chain and sends the sample to the spectrum analyzer. The main signal is not interrupted. On screen it appears as the **Tap Points** card. You choose where to look, and the analyzer shows the signal at that point.

![Tap Points card showing the selected tap point, its status and the coupling value.](/images/shots/equipment/coupler.webp)

## Where to find it

The Tap Points card is on the **RX Analysis** tab of Mission Control, below the Notch Filter card. See [RX Analysis](/mission-control/rx-analysis/).

## Default mode

By default there is one tap, and it can sit on one of two points:

| Control | What it does | Typical value |
|---|---|---|
| Tap Point (switch) | Turns the tap on or off. When off, the analyzer gets no signal from the coupler. | On |
| Tap Point (list) | Picks **TX IF** (the transmit IF going to the upconverter) or **RX IF** (the receive IF coming out of the LNB chain). | RX IF |
| Status | Active when the tap is on and signal can flow at that point. | Active |
| Coupling | How much the tap takes off the line. | -20 dB |

## Engineering mode

Engineering mode adds a second tap and eight tap points. Turn it on with the **Engineering Mode** button in the header. The button appears only in advanced scenarios.

With engineering mode on, the card shows **Tap Point A** and **Tap Point B**, each with its own switch, list, Status and Coupling. Tap A is -30 dB and starts off. Tap B is -20 dB and starts on. Each list offers:

| Tap point | Where it samples |
|---|---|
| TX IF | Transmit IF, before the BUC |
| TX RF POST BUC | After the BUC, before the HPA |
| TX RF POST HPA | After the HPA, before the OMT |
| TX RF POST OMT | After the OMT, at the antenna feed (transmit) |
| RX RF PRE OMT | At the antenna feed (receive), before the OMT |
| RX RF POST OMT | After the OMT, before the LNB |
| RX RF POST LNA | After the LNB's low-noise amplifier |
| RX IF | Receive IF, after the LNB |

A tap is Active only when it is switched on and its point carries signal. A transmit tap shows signal while the transmitter is sending. A receive tap shows signal while the receive chain is receiving.

## Common tasks

### Look at the receive IF

<Steps>

1. Open the **RX Analysis** tab.
2. On the Tap Points card, make sure the switch is on and the list reads **RX IF**.
3. Check that Status reads **Active**.
4. Read the signal on the spectrum analyzer. See [Real-Time Spectrum Analyzer](/equipment/real-time-spectrum-analyzer/).

</Steps>

### Check your own transmit signal

<Steps>

1. Choose **TX IF** in the list (or **TX RF POST HPA** in engineering mode).
2. Confirm the transmitter is sending on the [TX Chain](/mission-control/tx-chain/) tab.
3. Read the carrier on the analyzer.

</Steps>

### Compare two points

<Steps>

1. Turn on engineering mode.
2. Set Tap A to one point and Tap B to another, and switch both on.
3. Compare the two traces on the analyzer. The level difference shows the gain or loss of the equipment between the two points.

</Steps>

## Status and alarms

The coupler is passive and raises no alarms. The status light is green when a tap is on and its point has signal. It is off otherwise.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Analyzer shows only noise | The tap is off, or the chosen point has no signal. | Switch the tap on and choose a point on an active path. |
| Status is not Active on a TX point | The transmitter is not sending. | Start a carrier on the TX Chain tab. |
| Only two tap points are in the list | Engineering mode is off. | Turn on Engineering Mode in the header (advanced scenarios). |
| Analyzer level looks low | The tap takes only a small sample (-20 or -30 dB). | Read the level with the coupling value in mind. |
