The Orthomode Transducer (OMT), also called the duplexer, sits between the antenna feed and the rest of the RF front end. It sends transmit (TX) and receive (RX) signals down separate polarization paths and keeps the two apart. One polarization carries your uplink out. The other carries the downlink in.

The OMT is passive. There are no controls on it in normal operation. You read it to confirm that the polarization is what you expect and that isolation is healthy.

![OMT / Duplexer card showing TX and RX polarization, cross-pol isolation and status on the ACU Control tab.](/images/shots/equipment/orthomode-transducer.webp)

## Where to find it

The **OMT / Duplexer** card is on the **ACU Control** tab of Mission Control, below the tracking panel. See [ACU Control](/mission-control/acu-control/).

## Readouts and controls

| Readout or control | What it does | Typical value |
|---|---|---|
| TX Polarization | The polarization of the outgoing signal as it leaves the feed (H or V). It includes the antenna's skew. | H or V |
| RX Polarization | The polarization the receive path accepts, after skew. | The opposite of TX |
| Cross-Pol Isolation | How well the OMT keeps energy in the unwanted polarization out of the wanted path. Each unit has its own fixed value. | about 25 to 35 dB |
| Status | OK, or FAULT when isolation is below 20 dB. | OK |
| Reverse Polarization | Swaps the TX and RX polarizations. Shown only in engineering mode. | Off |

The Reverse Polarization switch is visible only when **Engineering Mode** is on. The Engineering Mode button in the header appears in advanced scenarios.

## Insertion loss

Like any piece of waveguide, the OMT loses a little power, nominally 0.5 dB. On transmit that loss comes off your uplink: the HPA output reaches the feed 0.5 dB lower. A fault can raise the loss above the nominal value. The extra loss costs the received carriers and adds noise on the receive path.

## Common tasks

### Check the polarization matches the satellite

<Steps>

1. Open the **ACU Control** tab.
2. Read **TX Polarization** and **RX Polarization** on the OMT / Duplexer card.
3. Compare them with the satellite's published polarization.
4. If they are swapped, adjust the antenna's Polarization (skew) on the same tab. In engineering mode, use **Reverse Polarization**.

</Steps>

### Judge the isolation

<Steps>

1. Read **Cross-Pol Isolation**. Higher is better.
2. Check **Status**. OK means isolation is at or above 20 dB.
3. If Status reads FAULT, expect weak received signals and leakage between paths.

</Steps>

## Status and alarms

- **OK**: isolation is 20 dB or more.
- **FAULT**: isolation has dropped below 20 dB. A signal in the wrong polarization arrives weakened by roughly the isolation value in dB.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Received carrier is weak, TX and RX look swapped | Polarization mismatch with the satellite. | Adjust the antenna Polarization; use Reverse Polarization in engineering mode. |
| Signal drops by a dB or two with no other change | The OMT has added insertion loss. | Report it as an equipment fault. |
| Status shows FAULT | Isolation is below 20 dB. | The value belongs to the unit and the polarization controls do not change it. Report it as a hardware fault. |
| Signal drops by about the isolation value | Cross-pol loss. | Fix the polarization first, then recheck. |
