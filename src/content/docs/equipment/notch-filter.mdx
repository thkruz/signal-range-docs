The notch filter removes a narrow slice of the IF band. Where the IF Filter Bank keeps one wide block of spectrum, a notch cuts a gap in it. Use it to take out a jammer or a stray carrier that sits close to your signal, without losing the signal itself.

It sits after the IF filter and before the AGC. It has three independent notch stages.

![Notch Filter card with three notch stages, each with an enable switch, center frequency, width and depth.](/images/shots/equipment/notch-filter.webp)

## Where to find it

The Notch Filter card is on the **RX Analysis** tab of Mission Control. See [RX Analysis](/mission-control/rx-analysis/).

## Controls

Each of the three stages has the same controls. A header badge shows whether that stage's applied settings are on.

| Control | What it does | Typical value |
|---|---|---|
| Power | Powers the whole notch filter. When off, the stages are bypassed and their fields read "--". | On |
| Enable | Turns one stage on or off. A new stage starts off. | Off until needed |
| Center Freq (MHz) | The IF frequency to cut. Range 950 to 2150 MHz. The arrow buttons step by 10 or 100 MHz. | Stages start at 1200, 1500 and 1800 MHz |
| Width (MHz) | The width of the cut. Range 0.1 to 50 MHz. | 1 MHz |
| Depth (dB) | How much the stage attenuates at its center. Range 1 to 60 dB. | 20 dB |
| Apply Changes | Commits the staged values of all three stages. | n/a |

The notch filter uses staged values. Changes you type or step stay pending until you press **Apply Changes**. The button highlights when something is waiting. A pending change survives a power toggle, so you do not lose it by mistake.

## Common tasks

### Notch out an interferer

<Steps>

1. Open the **RX Analysis** tab and find the interferer on the spectrum analyzer. Note its frequency and width.
2. On the Notch Filter card, set a stage's **Center Freq** to the interferer's frequency.
3. Set **Width** a little wider than the interferer, and **Depth** deep enough to bring it down (20 dB or more).
4. Turn the stage's **Enable** switch on.
5. Press **Apply Changes**.
6. Check the analyzer. The interferer should drop and your carrier should be unchanged.

</Steps>

### Check you have not cut your own signal

<Steps>

1. Look at the receiver's lock and C/N after you apply a notch.
2. If your carrier fell, the notch is overlapping it. Move the center frequency off your carrier, or reduce Width.

</Steps>

## How a notch affects a carrier

A notch removes more of a carrier the more of it overlaps. A notch centered on a narrow interferer takes the full depth off it. A notch that only covers the edge of a wide carrier takes off a small share. Keep the notch narrow when your own carrier is close.

## Status and alarms

- **Notch N and M overlap**: two enabled stages cover the same frequencies. Move one so they do not overlap.

The notch filter raises no other alarms.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The interferer did not drop | The change is still staged. | Press Apply Changes. |
| The interferer did not drop | The stage is not enabled, or the center is off the interferer. | Turn Enable on and re-center using the analyzer marker. |
| Your carrier also dropped | The notch overlaps it. | Narrow Width or move Center Freq. |
| Fields read "--" and cannot be edited | The notch filter is powered off. | Turn Power on. |
| Overlap alarm | Two stages cover the same band. | Separate them, or use one wider stage. |
