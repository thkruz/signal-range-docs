<Aside type="caution">
HPA back-off at 5 dB is marginal. With a 36 MHz QPSK carrier driven that close to

the coordination floor in RedSky's slot.

## Mitigation Approach

Back-off is the right lever here. It linearizes the amplifier, suppresses IMD by
roughly 2x the back-off delta in dBc, and does not require touching the customer
carrier or coordinating with SeaLink.

| Option | Trade-off | Recommended? |
|--------|-----------|--------------|
| Raise HPA back-off to 10 dB | Drops TX power ~5 dB, suppresses IMD substantially | **Yes** |
| Reduce TX modem power | Kills the customer link or drops margin below SLA | No |
| Retune carrier center | Requires SeaLink coordination and a service window | No |
| Add an RX notch at 5,961 MHz | Protects our receiver, does nothing for the neighbor | No |

The customer link must remain operational throughout. SeaLink does not need to
know this happened.

## Coordination Reply

A correct reply to RedSky looks like:

> *"Confirmed clear. TIDEMARK-3 TP-1 carrier holds 5,967 - 6,003 MHz H-pol with
> adjacent-channel emissions well below coordination floor in your planned slot.
> Proceed with 5,961 MHz V-pol uplink at 0937 as scheduled."*

What it does **not** look like:

- *"We have muted to give you the spectrum."* (Wrong - SeaLink is still up.)
- *"Cannot confirm - recommend delay."* (Wrong - the mitigation is local and quick.)
- *"We are retuning to give wider margin."* (Wrong - back-off is the right tool, not retune.)

State your occupied band. Confirm cleanliness. Clear them to proceed. Done.

## Documentation

Frequency-coordination events are logged so the next shift inherits the
back-off change and the coordination commitment. A correct entry captures:

- Time and station
- Partner identity and reference number
- Frequencies (ours and theirs)
- Action taken and reasoning
- Reply sent

Example:

> *"0937 - VT-01. RedSky coordination notice RST-COORD-2026-0331 received and
> confirmed. TIDEMARK-3 TP-1 HPA back-off raised from 5 to 10 dB to suppress
> adjacent-channel IMD into RedSky's planned 5,957 - 5,965 MHz V-pol slot.
> SeaLink carrier remains nominal. RedSky cleared for 0937 uplink."*

If RedSky's carrier behaves unexpectedly after they come up, the next operator
will know we touched the back-off and why.

## End of Briefing

This is good neighbor work. No customer is in pain, no equipment is broken, no
supervisor is escalating. A partner teleport is being courteous enough to tell
us they are about to use spectrum near ours, and they expect us to be courteous
enough to confirm we will not step on them.

Verify the math. Inspect the TX chain. Move the back-off lever. Confirm the
customer is fine. Send the reply. Log it.

Dana will read the log entry. RedSky's coordinator will read the reply. Neither
of them will say anything about it - and that is the point.
