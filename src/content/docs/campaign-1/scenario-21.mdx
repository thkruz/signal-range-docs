## Interference Discrimination Matrix (SOP-IX-004)

The single most valuable question: **is the interference on the transponder, or
is it local to our site?**

| Test | Seen at ME-02 only | Seen by every station on TP-1 |
|------|--------------------|-------------------------------|
| Implication | Terrestrial / local RX problem | The interferer is in the **uplink** - it is being relayed by the satellite |
| Examples | Radar, 5G OOB emissions, our own equipment | Misaligned partner uplink, carrier-under-carrier, deliberate uplink jamming |
| Cross-check | Compare with VT-01 / Halifax view of the same transponder | Same |

Then the signature questions, in order of diagnostic power:

1. **Duty cycle.** Natural and accidental sources are continuous or random.
   Regular on/off timing (e.g., minutes on, minutes off, repeating) implies
   either rotating machinery (radar - seconds, not minutes) or a hand on a
   transmit switch.
2. **Bandwidth and shape.** Cross-pol leakage mirrors a known neighbor's
   carrier. An errant uplink looks like a modulated carrier. Broadband noise
   centered on OUR allocation, sized to OUR carrier, matches nothing in the
   coordination database - that is what deliberate denial looks like.
3. **Frequency behavior.** Drifting = cheap oscillator (accidental). Parked
   dead-center on the victim carrier = aimed.
4. **Correlation with our traffic.** Interference that starts when your carrier
   does, or follows a retune, is watching you.

<Aside type="caution">
Discrimination is a confidence call, not a verdict. The log language is
"signature consistent with deliberate uplink interference" - attribution (who,
from where) belongs to the regulator and the geolocation providers, not the
ground operator. What you own is the evidence quality.
</Aside>

## Denial vs Intrusion (security reference)

Jamming is a **denial** attack on the RF layer. It does not touch the data
layer. Two separate verifications, never conflated:

- [ ] RF layer: interference characterized, C/N impact quantified
- [ ] Data layer: crypto status ACTIVE, key status Valid, no unexplained frame
      events outside the jamming windows

If the crypto state changes during an RF incident, you no longer have an
interference event - you have a security incident, and the escalation path
changes (Dana immediately, then the security officer; nothing gets power-cycled
that holds keys).

## Receive-Side Mitigation Options

| Option | When | Cost |
|--------|------|------|
| Notch filter on the interferer | Narrowband interferer inside our passband | Notch bandwidth comes out of our carrier's SNR |
| Tighten IF filtering | Interferer at passband edge | Slight group-delay penalty |
| Ride it out, document windows | Duty-cycled interferer, link survives the bursts | Customer sees periodic margin dips |

There is no transmit-side mitigation for uplink jamming available to a ground
station: power escalation ("burn through") degrades neighbors, violates
coordination, and feeds the incident. Do not raise power.

## Regulator Report Fields (ITU-R / national authority practice)

Build the package as you observe; every field timestamped:

- Victim: satellite, transponder, polarization, center frequency, bandwidth
- Interference: center frequency, bandwidth, measured duty cycle, first/last seen
- Evidence: spectrum captures at documented analyzer settings
- Cross-station confirmation: which other sites see it (transponder vs local)
- Service impact: C/N degradation, demod events, customer effect
- Mitigation applied and its cost
