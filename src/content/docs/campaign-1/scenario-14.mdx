**Current Conditions (1318):**
- Temperature: 54°F (12°C)
- Wind: W at 8 mph
- Precipitation: None yet
- Visibility: 10 miles

**Forecast (1325-1545):**
- Moderate rain front passing overhead, ~20 minutes peak
- No freezing - temperatures stay well above 32°F
- Expected link margin impact: ~3 dB fade at peak
- AGC compensation range available

**Operational Considerations:**

Rain attenuates C-band signals roughly in proportion to rain intensity along the
slant path. A 3 dB fade is well within AGC compensation range for a healthy link.
The feed heater is not just for ice - it also keeps water from beading and sheeting
on the feed assembly. Dry surfaces attenuate less than wet ones. Bring it up
before the rain lands.

## Handover Decision Framework

The customer prefers no handover. The customer does not get the last word on RF
reality. The standing trigger conditions for flipping the call from "hold" to
"hand off" are operational, not contractual.

### Hold When

- AGC has headroom remaining (compensation in use is well below max).
- Modem lock is maintained.
- Beacon C/N is above the watch threshold.
- Customer has stated SLA preference for hold.
- A fallback station is not available without impact (ME-02 is busy on TM-2).

### Hand Off When

- AGC is at or near max compensation **and** fade is still deepening.
- Modem drops lock for more than a brief dip.
- C/N falls below the action threshold and is not recovering.
- The forecast is going to outlast AGC's remaining cushion.

<Aside type="caution">
"Hold" is a positive decision, not an absence of decision. State the trigger
conditions you are watching for so you know when to flip. That discipline is what
distinguishes holding from inertia.
</Aside>

## HPA Back-off During a Fade

It is technically possible to reduce HPA back-off to push more uplink power. For a
3 dB rain fade with downlink AGC compensating cleanly, **don't.**

| Action | Benefit | Cost |
|--------|---------|------|
| Reduce back-off from 10 dB to 7 dB | +3 dB uplink margin you don't need | IMD into adjacent transponders; amplifier stress; spurious emissions risk |
| Hold back-off at 10 dB | Nominal IMD; nominal amplifier wear | None - link is healthy without it |

Optimization is not "turn every dial to max." If the link has margin you don't need,
the responsible move is to not spend it. Keep back-off nominal through the front.

## Escalation Guidelines

### Handle Yourself

- Feed heater enable.
- AGC and link margin monitoring.
- The hold-or-hand-off decision.
- Customer notification on a successful hold.

### Loop In Dana

- Modem dropping lock during the fade.
- AGC reaching max compensation while the front is still building.
- A second weather event arriving before this one clears.
- Customer escalation (James calling rather than emailing).

Dana is on-site this shift, not on-call - she would rather you check in mid-fade
than discover after the fact that you held a link you shouldn't have.

## Documentation

A weather event that the operator chose to ride out must be logged so the next
shift knows the call was deliberate and what triggers were being watched. A correct
entry captures:

- Time, station, weather event characterization.
- Customer SLA preference (hold) and that it was honored.
- Peak AGC compensation in use; minimum C/N observed.
- The handover triggers that were being watched but did not fire.
- Whether the customer was notified post-event.

Example:

> *"1325-1545 - Moderate rain over VT-01, ~3 dB peak fade. Held TM-1 service per
> SeaLink SLA preference. Peak AGC gain 3 dB (of 10 dB available), beacon C/N
> minimum 7.8 dB. Triggers watched: AGC at max with continued fade; modem unlock.
> Neither fired. SeaLink notified by reply on customer portal."*

If the hold had failed - if the call had flipped to handover - the same entry
records the trigger that fired and the time it did.

## End of Briefing

S3 taught you that when the weather is bad enough, you hand the link off. S14 is
the other half of the lesson: when the weather is not that bad, you don't.

Escape is a tool, not a reflex. The customer told you what they value. The link
will tell you whether their preference is operationally supportable. Match the
two.

Bring the feed heater up before the rain lands. Watch the margins. Hold or hand
off on the trigger conditions, not on the customer's wishes alone.
