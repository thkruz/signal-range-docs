## Weather Briefing

**Current Conditions (1318):**
- Temperature: 54°F (12°C)
- Wind: W at 8 mph
- Precipitation: None yet
- Visibility: 10 miles

**Forecast (1320-1340):**
- Heavy convective rain cell passing overhead (peak around 50 mm/h), ~20 minutes through
- No freezing - temperatures stay well above 32°F
- Expected link margin impact: about 1 dB of C/N at the heaviest part of the cell (C-band)
- AGC compensation range available

**Operational Considerations:**

At C-band rain barely attenuates the carrier: a heavy cell takes only a few tenths of a dB
off the signal. Most of what you will see is noise: the wet path radiates at about 275 K into the
beam and lifts the system noise temperature, so C/N drops by about 1 dB while the input
level moves well under a dB. The same cell would take several dB off a Ku link. Either way it is well
within AGC compensation range for a healthy link.

**What the cell looks like on the console.** It is small, so know where to look:

- RX modem 1 Raw C/N on the TIDEMARK-1 carrier (RX Analysis): about 13 dB before the rain,
  about 1 dB lower with the cell overhead (single reads can dip a little further). That is not
  much more than the readout's normal wander, which is why you write the baseline down first. This is the
  readout the action threshold below is measured on. The modem loses that full 1 dB because our own
  uplink to TIDEMARK-1 is rained on too.
- ACU Environmental card: Precipitation ACTIVE while the cell is overhead, and the beacon
  C/N on the ACU down by about 1 dB.
- The RAIN FADE info alarm: it shows once the fade passes 0.5 dB, about 2 minutes into the
  cell, and clears as the cell passes. Its detail reads the downlink loss alone, about 0.7 dB at
  the peak, so it sits a little under the modem's 1 dB dip. The fade steps (the Raw C/N watch, then
  the AGC and HPA reads) wait for this alarm: until it is up, the cell is not overhead yet.
- AGC gain: it moves by under 1 dB as the cell comes over, because the AGC follows
  the total passband power and rain barely moves that. What matters is that it stays far below its
  maximum.

The cell takes about twenty minutes to pass. Once your hold is set up and the link is
holding, you can wait it out or skip ahead with the skip control in the command bar. The skip
jumps to the moment the cell clears and the weather runs its course on the way. While the hold
step waits, the mission clock is held (HOLD beside the timer), so waiting it out and skipping
score the same.

The feed heater is not just for ice - it also keeps water from beading and sheeting
on the feed assembly. Dry surfaces attenuate less than wet ones. Bring it up
before the rain arrives. (The ACU card reads "Prevents ice, keeps the feed dry"; it is one
heater doing both jobs. The Rain Blower is optional at C-band, even in a heavy cell, and not part of this procedure.)

## Handover Decision Framework

The customer prefers no handover. The customer does not get the last word on RF
reality. The standing trigger conditions for flipping the call from "hold" to
"hand off" are operational, not contractual.

### Hold When

- AGC has headroom remaining (compensation in use is well below max).
- Modem lock is maintained.
- RX modem 1 Raw C/N is above the 7 dB action threshold.
- Customer has stated SLA preference for hold.
- A fallback station is not available without impact (ME-02 is busy on TM-2).

### Hand Off When

- AGC is at or near max compensation **and** fade is still deepening.
- Modem drops lock for more than a brief dip.
- RX modem 1 Raw C/N falls below 7 dB (the action threshold) and is not recovering.
- The forecast is going to outlast AGC's remaining cushion.

<Aside type="caution">
"Hold" is a positive decision, not an absence of decision. State the trigger
conditions you are watching for so you know when to flip. That discipline is what
distinguishes holding from inertia.
</Aside>

## HPA Back-off During a Fade

It is technically possible to reduce HPA back-off to push more uplink power. For a
1 dB C-band rain fade with downlink AGC compensating cleanly, **don't.**

| Action | Benefit | Cost |
|--------|---------|------|
| Reduce back-off from 10 dB to 7 dB | +3 dB uplink power (50 -> 53 dBm, 100 -> 200 W) you don't need | IM3 about 6 dB worse (roughly 2 dB per dB given up) into adjacent transponders; amplifier stress; spurious emissions risk |
| Hold back-off at 10 dB | Nominal IMD; nominal amplifier wear | None - link is healthy without it |

Optimization is not "turn every dial to max." If the link has margin you don't need,
the responsible move is to not spend it. Keep back-off nominal through the front.

## Monitoring Checklist

### Before the Front (1318-1320)

- [ ] Feed heater enabled
- [ ] Dashboard: no warning or critical alarms (the Signal(s) Detected info row is normal)
- [ ] RX modem 1 Raw C/N noted at baseline (write it down: about 13 dB)
- [ ] Modem locked with margin (Raw C/N 12 dB or more, the nominal band)

### Through the Fade (1320-1340)

- [ ] Hold until the cell has passed (wait it out or skip ahead)
- [ ] Receiver lock maintained
- [ ] RX modem 1 Raw C/N held above 7 dB (action threshold)
- [ ] AGC gain far below max (it moves under 1 dB with the cell)
- [ ] The ticker shows "RAIN FADE" as an info alarm once the fade passes 0.5 dB, about 2 minutes into the cell (its detail reads the downlink loss, about 0.7 dB at the peak, mostly sky noise; the modem's C/N drops about 1 dB because our own uplink is rained on too); it clears as the cell passes
- [ ] Antenna still locked on TIDEMARK-1
- [ ] HPA back-off unchanged at 10 dB

### After the Front

- [ ] C/N recovering toward baseline
- [ ] AGC gain backing down
- [ ] SeaLink told on the customer portal that service held (recorded in the log entry)
- [ ] Log entry captures decision, thresholds watched, recovery

## Documentation

A weather event that the operator chose to ride out must be logged so the next
shift knows the call was deliberate and what triggers were being watched. A correct
entry captures:

- Time, station, weather event characterization.
- Customer SLA preference (hold) and that it was honored.
- Peak AGC compensation in use; minimum C/N observed.
- The handover triggers that were being watched but did not fire (RX modem 1 Raw C/N
  under 7 dB, AGC at max with continued fade, modem unlock).
- That the customer was notified post-event.

Example:

> *"1320-1340 - Heavy rain cell over VT-01, about 1 dB of C/N lost at peak (mostly sky
> noise). Held TM-1 service per SeaLink SLA preference. AGC gain far below max (under 1 dB
> of movement), RX modem 1 Raw C/N about 1 dB under its 13 dB baseline at worst. Triggers
> watched: Raw C/N under 7 dB; AGC at max with continued fade; modem unlock. None fired.
> SeaLink notified by reply on customer portal."*

If the hold had failed - if the call had flipped to handover - the same entry
records the trigger that fired and the time it did.
