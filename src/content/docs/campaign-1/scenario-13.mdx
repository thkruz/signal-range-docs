## BUC Operating Reference

### Nominal Ranges

| Parameter | Nominal | Caution | Alarm Trip |
|-----------|---------|---------|------------|
| Temperature | 35-55°C | 55-70°C | > 70°C |
| Current Draw | 2.5-3.2 A | 3.2-4.5 A | > 4.5 A |
| Gain | 23 dB (operating value) | > 25 dB - investigate | n/a |

### Heat Source

The BUC dissipates electrical power as heat in proportion to its drive level minus its useful RF output. Excess gain that the HPA does not need turns into chassis heat instead of useful radiated power. Higher current + higher temperature with no other anomaly is one story, not two.

<Aside type="caution">
The 70°C trip will mute the BUC automatically and drop the carrier hard. If the trend reaches the trip point, the decision has been made for you - and the customer notices.
</Aside>

## Response Options

Four operational responses are available for a pre-alarm thermal trend. Each has a cost. Choose the one that matches the situation, not the most aggressive option available.

| Option | What It Does | Customer Cost | When It Fits |
|--------|--------------|---------------|--------------|
| **De-rate** | Reduce BUC gain ~10 dB, let HPA backoff increase, monitor trend reverse | None (within SLA margin) | Trend is real, root cause is excess gain, time is available |
| **Swap now** | Take carrier down hard, hot-swap BUC module | Full outage during swap | BUC is failing fast or already past trip |
| **Mute + switch backup** | Mute primary, transition to backup modem chain | Brief carrier interruption | Primary is suspect across the chain, not just BUC |
| **Hold and monitor** | Take no action, watch the curve | None unless trip | Trend is flattening on its own (it is not, here) |

---

## De-Rate Sequence

If the de-rate is the right call, execute it live - no mute, no theater:

1. Lower BUC gain in a single adjustment (target 23 dB operating value, down from 33 dB)
2. Verify HPA still in linear region (backoff increases as drive drops)
3. Verify BUC not saturated
4. Watch 5-10 minutes: slope flattens, then trends down, current draw normalizes
5. Open maintenance ticket for BUC swap on next planned window
6. Log the action

## Escalation Guidelines

### Handle Yourself

- Pre-alarm thermal trends with clear root cause
- BUC gain adjustments within the operating range
- Maintenance ticket creation for planned-window swaps
- Customer carriers remaining within SLA margin throughout

### Notify Dana

- Trend reaches the 70°C trip with no time to react
- Carrier degrades below SLA floor during any adjustment
- Multiple unrelated anomalies appear during the response
- You judge a hot swap or emergency switchover is warranted

Dana is in the back office. She does not need to be flagged for routine in-shift judgment calls. She does need to be flagged before anything affects the customer.

---

## Maintenance Ticket Reference

Tickets are handoffs. The next person reads them cold. Include:

- The trend record (timestamps, temperatures, currents - the curve, not just the verdict)
- The action you took on-shift (de-rate to specific gain, time of action)
- The recommendation (swap module during next planned VT-01 maintenance window)
- Carrier impact (none / minor / outage)

A ticket that says "BUC running hot" is not a ticket. It is a complaint.
