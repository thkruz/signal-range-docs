## Manual Operations Procedure (SOP-MN-001)

- [ ] Acknowledge the automation fault; confirm servos/manual mode healthy
- [ ] Record current position BEFORE touching anything (the dish is on the bird - do not lose that)
- [ ] Switch tracking mode to MANUAL deliberately (a faulted controller in a half-automatic state is worse than honest manual). **Timed: running out fails the shift**
- [ ] Confirm/refine pointing against the prediction sheet
- [ ] Prove pointing: beacon at 1,074.5 MHz IF (**timed: running out fails the shift**), then carrier lock at the receiver
- [ ] Hold and spot-check periodically; GEO drift over one shift is negligible
- [ ] Log the timeline in the Ops Log (type it): fault time, manual entry, every position input, IT updates, and that the ACU was not rebooted

### Why Not Just Reboot It?

The controller holds calibration state. An uncoordinated power cycle can come
back with stale or defaulted axis calibration - and IT loses the crash state
they need for root cause. Bypass first, stabilize the service, let IT do
diagnosis on their schedule. (The same rule as power-cycling an LNB: you cycle
equipment YOU own; you coordinate cycles on equipment someone else is debugging.)
