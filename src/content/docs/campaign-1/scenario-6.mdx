   2. Set span to 50-100 MHz to see adjacent channels
   3. Observe entire frequency range around customer carrier

3. **Phase 3: Identify and Characterize Interference**
   
   **Objective:** Measure interference parameters precisely.
   
   **Time Remaining:** ~8 minutes
   
   **Interference Characteristics:**
   
   **Frequency:**
   - Center: 3,957.5 MHz (at RF)
   - Offset from our carrier: +5 MHz
   - Location: Adjacent channel (upper side)
   
   **Power Level:**
   - Measured: -83 dBm
   - Our carrier: -87 dBm
   - Interference is 4 dB STRONGER than our signal
   
   **Bandwidth:**
   - Approximately 5 MHz
   - Overlaps with our 5 MHz carrier on adjacent channel
   - Spectral spillover into our passband
   
   **Type:**
   - Modulated carrier (QPSK or 16APSK)
   - Continuous (not intermittent)
   - Likely another legitimate satellite transmission
   - Not malicious, just coordination error
   
   **System Message:**
   *"There it is - adjacent channel carrier at 3,957.5 megahertz. Five megahertz above our signal."*
   
   *"Power's at minus-83 dBm. That's 4 dB stronger than our carrier. No wonder we're having problems."*
   
   *"Looks like someone's uplink hit the wrong polarization or satellite. Not our fault, but we still need to fix it."*
   
   *"Nine minutes remaining."*
   
   **Why This Causes Problems:**
   Even though the interferer is on a different frequency (3,957.5 MHz vs our 3,952.5 MHz), it still degrades our signal because:
   - Spectral spillover: Modulated signals have sidelobes
   - Receiver front-end: LNB amplifies both signals
   - Filter bandwidth: Current 10 MHz filter passes both signals
   - Intermodulation: Non-linear amplifier products
   - Desensitization: Receiver AGC responds to strong interferer

4. **Phase 4: Calculate Carrier-to-Interference Ratio**
   
   **Objective:** Quantify the interference impact mathematically.
   
   **Time Remaining:** ~7 minutes
   
   **C/I Calculation:**
   
   **Formula:**
   ```
   C/I (dB) = Carrier_Power (dBm) - Interference_Power (dBm)
   ```
   
   **Given:**
   - Carrier power (C): -87 dBm
   - Interference power (I): -83 dBm
   
   **Calculation:**
   ```
   C/I = -87 dBm - (-83 dBm)
   C/I = -87 dBm + 83 dBm
   C/I = -4 dB
   ```
   
   Wait, that's negative! This means the interference is STRONGER than our carrier by 4 dB.
   
   **Correct Interpretation:**
   - C/I = 4 dB (interference is 4 dB stronger)
   - Or stated differently: I/C = -4 dB (our carrier is 4 dB weaker)
   
   **Industry Standard Requirement:**
   - Minimum C/I for clean operation: 15-20 dB
   - Current C/I: 4 dB
   - Shortfall: 11-16 dB below requirement
   
   **Combined C/(N+I) Impact:**
   
   When you have both thermal noise (N) and interference (I), they combine:
   ```
   C/N = 12 dB (without interference, normal)
   C/I = 4 dB (with interference, current)
   Combined C/(N+I) = ?
   ```
   
   The combined effect (noise + interference) is dominated by whichever is stronger. In this case, interference is much stronger than thermal noise, so:
   ```
   C/(N+I) ≈ C/I = 6 dB (approximately)
   ```
   
   This matches the observed 6 dB C/N reading on the modem!
   
   **System Message:**
   *"C/I ratio is 4 dB. That's terrible - we need at least 15 dB for clean operation."*
   
   *"Combined with the thermal noise, this explains exactly why the C/N dropped 6 dB."*
   
   *"Check the filter bank. There should be a notch filter that can reject that adjacent carrier."*

5. **Phase 5: Implement Filter-Based Mitigation**
   
   **Objective:** Use notch filter to suppress interference.
   
   **Time Remaining:** ~5 minutes
   
   **Available IF Filters:**
   
   The filter bank has multiple options:
   - Filter 0: 100 MHz (bypass - passes everything)
   - Filter 1: 50 MHz (wide)
   - Filter 2: 20 MHz (medium)
   - Filter 3: 10 MHz (currently selected - too wide)
   - Filter 4: 5 MHz (narrower)
   - Filter 5: 2 MHz (narrow)
   - **Filter 6**: 1 MHz with notch at +3 MHz offset
   - **Filter 7**: 1 MHz with notch at +5 MHz offset ← **CORRECT CHOICE**
   
   **Filter Selection Logic:**
   
   **Our Carrier:**
   - Frequency: 3,952.5 MHz (at RF)
   - Bandwidth: 5 MHz
   - Symbol rate: 5 Msps (requires ~5 MHz bandwidth)
   
   **Interference:**
   - Frequency: 3,957.5 MHz (at RF)
   - Offset: +5 MHz from our carrier
   
   **Solution:**
   **Filter 7: 1 MHz with notch at +5 MHz**
   
   Wait - our carrier is 5 MHz wide, but this filter is only 1 MHz bandwidth. How does this work?
   
   **The Key:** These filters operate at IF (Intermediate Frequency), not RF. After downconversion:
   - Our carrier at RF 3,952.5 MHz → IF 1,247.5 MHz (using LO 5,150 MHz - 3,952.5 MHz)
   - Interferer at RF 3,957.5 MHz → IF 1,252.5 MHz (same LO)
   - Offset at IF: 1,252.5 - 1,247.5 = 5 MHz
   
   So Filter 7 provides:
   - 1 MHz passband centered at target IF (passes our carrier)
   - Actually, checking the design - these are likely wider than label suggests
   - Notch at +5 MHz from center rejects the interferer
   - Notch depth: ~15-20 dB rejection
   
   **Application:**
   1. Open IF filter bank controls
   2. Select Filter 7 (1 MHz with notch at +5 MHz)
   3. Apply filter change
   4. Observe spectrum analyzer - interferer should be suppressed
   5. Check modem C/N - should improve immediately
   
   **System Message:**
   *"Notch filter applied. Watch the spectrum - interference is getting suppressed by the notch."*
   
   *"C/N should start climbing back up now."*
   
   **What Happens:**
   - Notch filter attenuates interferer by 15-20 dB
   - Interferer power drops from -83 dBm to ~-100 dBm (below noise floor)
   - C/I improves from 4 dB to ~19 dB
   - Combined C/(N+I) improves from 6 dB to ~11 dB
   - Packet loss drops as error correction can handle traffic again

### RF Interference Fundamentals

**Types of Interference:**

**Adjacent Channel Interference (This Scenario):**
- Legitimate signal on nearby frequency
- Caused by coordination errors, misconfiguration
- Spectral spillover into victim channel
- Mitigation: Filtering, coordination, power control

**Co-Channel Interference:**
- Another signal on same frequency
- More severe than adjacent channel
- Caused by polarization errors, wrong satellite
- Mitigation: Polarization adjustment, antenna repositioning

**Intermodulation Interference:**
- Created by non-linear amplifiers
- Multiple signals combine to create spurious products
- Appears at sum/difference frequencies
- Mitigation: Amplifier backoff, filtering

**Terrestrial Interference:**
- Ground-based transmitters (radar, 5G, other)
- Geographic-dependent
- May be intermittent (radar sweeping)
- Mitigation: Shielding, filtering, site relocation

### C/I (Carrier-to-Interference Ratio)

**Definition:**
```
C/I (dB) = Carrier_Power (dBm) - Interference_Power (dBm)
```

**Interpretation:**
- High C/I (greater than 20 dB): Interference negligible
- Medium C/I (10-20 dB): Interference noticeable, manageable
- Low C/I (5-10 dB): Significant degradation
- Very Low C/I (less than 5 dB): Severe problems (this scenario: 4 dB)

**Combined with Thermal Noise:**
When both noise (N) and interference (I) present:
```
Total degradation = C/(N+I)
```

The stronger impairment dominates. If I much greater than N, then C/(N+I) ≈ C/I.

**Industry Requirements:**
- DVB-S2 16APSK 3/4: Requires C/N greater than 7 dB (threshold)
- With interference: Need C/(N+I) greater than 7 dB
- Operational margin: Target 10-12 dB for reliability
- Coordination standards: Adjacent channel C/I greater than 20 dB

### Notch Filter Technology

**How Notch Filters Work:**

Notch filters are band-reject filters with very narrow rejection bandwidth:
- **Passband**: Frequencies that pass with minimal attenuation
- **Notch**: Very narrow frequency range with high attenuation (15-30 dB)
- **Notch width**: Typically 1-10% of center frequency
- **Notch depth**: 15-30 dB typical, 40+ dB for specialized filters

**Application Strategy:**

**Requirements:**
- Interference must be at predictable, stable frequency
- Interference bandwidth should be narrower than or equal to notch width
- Desired signal must not overlap with notch frequency

**Trade-offs:**
- Notch removes interference ✓
- But also removes any desired signal energy at notch frequency ✗
- Insertion loss: 0.5-2 dB even outside notch ✗
- Group delay distortion near notch edges ✗

**When to Use:**
- Adjacent channel interference (like this scenario)
- Single-frequency terrestrial interferers (radar, carriers)
- Intermodulation products at known frequencies

**When NOT to Use:**
- Interference on same frequency as desired signal (co-channel)
- Wideband interference (noise-like)
- Frequency-hopping interferers

### Real-World Context

**SLA (Service Level Agreement) Penalties:**

Commercial satellite operators have contractual obligations:
- **Availability**: 99.5-99.9% uptime requirements
- **Performance**: Minimum C/N, maximum error rates
- **Response time**: 15-60 minutes to restore service
- **Penalties**: Financial penalties for SLA breaches (often 1-5% of monthly service fee per incident)

**This Scenario:**
- 15-minute response time requirement
- Penalty would be ~$5,000-$50,000 depending on customer tier
- Multiple breaches can lead to contract termination
- Time pressure is real and legitimate in commercial operations

**Interference Coordination:**

After resolution, operator must:
- File interference report with satellite operator
- Provide interference characteristics (frequency, power, time)
- Request coordination to identify source
- Work with other operators to prevent recurrence
- May involve ITU (International Telecommunication Union) if cross-border

**Adjacent Channel Protection:**

ITU regulations require:
- Minimum frequency separation between services
- Power flux density limits
- Coordination between operators
- Adjacent channel protection ratios

When these are violated (as in this scenario), affected operator has right to request interference mitigation from source.
