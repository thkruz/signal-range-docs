## Sun Transit Prediction Sheet (VT-01 / TIDEMARK-1)

**Source:** NATS ephemeris service, issued 0400 local

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Event | Solar conjunction - Sun crosses antenna boresight |
| Window open | ~1032:30 local |
| Peak | ~1035:00 local |
| Window close | ~1037:30 local |
| Predicted peak degradation | ~16 dB C/N (noise rise) |
| Expected demod impact | Loss of carrier lock for 1-3 minutes around peak |
| Season | Spring transit series, day 2 of 4 (1-4 March) |
| Geometry | TIDEMARK-1 at 53° W: Az 153.1°, El 35.1° from VT-01 |
| Tomorrow | About the same time; the Sun passes nearly dead center, so slightly deeper |

The Sun reaches TIDEMARK-1's azimuth and elevation about an hour and a half before local
solar noon (the bird sits east of our meridian), and only on the few days, a couple of weeks
before the equinox, when the Sun's declination matches the satellite's as seen from Vermont.
A GEO satellite does not move in our sky, so each day of the series falls at nearly the same
clock time - the window shifts by seconds, not minutes.

The Sun is a ~20,000 K wideband noise source at C-band. When it passes directly
behind TIDEMARK-1 as seen from our dish, the antenna stares into it at full gain
and the system noise temperature briefly swamps the link budget. No equipment is
at fault, nothing is broken, and no operator action changes the geometry.

<Aside type="note">
Transits happen twice a year per satellite per station, around the equinoxes - a
few minutes a day for several consecutive days. The exact window differs by
station: ME-02 sees its TM-2 transit at a slightly different time than we see
ours on TM-1. This is why a handover does not help: you would be trading a
predicted, brief, contractually excused outage for handover risk - and the sister
station inherits its own transit anyway.
</Aside>

## Sun Transit Procedure (SOP-SX-001)

### Before the Window (now - 1032)

- [ ] Dashboard: no warning or critical alarms (the Signal(s) Detected info row is normal)
- [ ] RX Analysis: beacon present, receiver locked, note baseline C/N (at least 10 dB)
- [ ] Customer notification sent (template below) - **before** the window opens
- [ ] Confirm uplink configuration: no changes planned during the window
- [ ] Stand by until the window opens (10:32:30 on the station clock). You can wait or use
      the skip control in the command bar; the mission clock is held while you wait

### During the Window (1032 - 1037)

- [ ] Do **not** repoint, do not handover, do not adjust the RX chain
- [ ] Keep the uplink exactly as it is - the satellite still hears us fine
- [ ] Watch the sky-noise alarm rise, peak, and fall on the alarm ticker at the top of the screen. Two alarms can show in the window: "SUN TRANSIT: ELEVATED SKY NOISE", with its value in the detail and tooltip and its colour following the noise (info under 2 dB, warning 2-6 dB, error 6 dB and up), and, while the demod is out, "VT-01(RX1): Carrier lock lost" (red). "Degraded margin" may flicker on either side of the lock loss. ACU Control's Sky Temp readout climbs with it. That is the event working as predicted
- [ ] Expect demod loss of lock near peak; expect it to recover on its own after the peak (10:35:00) as the noise falls

### After the Window (1037+)

- [ ] Receiver re-locked, C/N back at baseline
- [ ] No warning or critical alarms
- [ ] Impact documented: actual window, peak degradation, lock-loss duration (the Ops Log holds the
      lock-loss times as the "ALARM raised: ... Carrier lock lost" and "ALARM cleared: ... Carrier lock lost" lines)
- [ ] Marcus confirms spacecraft-side view matches ours

<Aside type="caution">
**The uplink is unaffected.** Sun transit raises OUR receive noise; the
satellite's view of our carrier never changes. Muting the BUC or disabling the
HPA during a transit creates a real outage on top of a cosmetic one - and the
RF-safety sequencing rules still apply if you touch the chain. The correct
amount of transmit-side action during a sun transit is none.
</Aside>

## Customer Notification Template

> *"SeaLink ops - predicted solar transit on TIDEMARK-1 from our Vermont
> station, [window open]-[window close] local today. Expect degraded margin and
> a possible 1-3 minute carrier interruption near [peak]. Service recovers
> without intervention. Per SLA section 7.2 this is an excluded event with
> advance notice - this message constitutes that notice."*

Send it before the window. A notification timestamped after the event starts is
worth nothing contractually and less than nothing for trust.

## Why Not Hand Over? (Reference)

| Option | Outcome |
|--------|---------|
| Ride through | 1-3 min predicted demod loss, SLA-excluded, zero risk |
| Handover to ME-02 | Handover event on the customer's contract dashboard, ME-02 takes its own transit on its own schedule, and the traffic comes back afterward - two transfers to dodge three minutes |
| Repoint / chase | The Sun is behind the satellite; any pointing change makes the link worse and risks losing the bird |

Predictable degradation you plan around. Unpredictable degradation you fight.
Sun transit is the textbook case of the first kind.

## Escalation Guidelines

### Handle Yourself

- Pre-event notification and baseline documentation
- Riding through the window with no configuration changes
- Post-event verification and impact report

### Inform Dana

- Lock does not recover within 5 minutes of window close
- Any alarm during the window other than "SUN TRANSIT: ELEVATED SKY NOISE" or the modem's "Carrier lock lost" / "Degraded margin"
- Actual peak degradation grossly exceeds the prediction (suggests something else is also wrong)

## End of Briefing

The hardest part of this shift is doing nothing while a customer link degrades
in front of you. The discipline is knowing the difference between a problem and
an event: problems get fixed, events get managed.

Notify before, hold during, verify and document after.
