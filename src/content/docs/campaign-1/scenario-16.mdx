## Recovery Priority Framework

When multiple unrelated faults converge, the order of operations matters more than the
speed of any individual fix. The standing framework is:

1. **RF Safety First** - Stop anything that is putting a dirty signal on the air or
   damaging equipment. An overdriven HPA spills IMD products into neighboring
   transponders and stresses the amplifier. An over-temperature BUC running unmuted
   continues to heat and risks failure.
2. **Customer Impact Next** - Restore the customer's receive path. Once you are not
   actively making things worse, get the link healthy again.
3. **Equipment Health and Verification** - Methodically validate each subsystem is back
   within spec. Confirm the alarm board is clean before notifying the customer.

For this incident the order resolves to:

- Disable the HPA output (takes the overdriven uplink off the air immediately).
- Mute the BUC (begins thermal recovery - safe now that the HPA is down).
- Restore HPA back-off to 10 dB while the chain is cold.
- Wait for BUC temperature and current to return to nominal.
- Power cycle the LNB to clear the sticky reference-lock fault.
- Verify receiver lock and C/N on TIDEMARK-1.
- Unmute the BUC, then re-enable the HPA, and confirm clean TX output.
- Final dashboard sweep, customer notification, log entry.

<Aside type="caution">
**Sequencing rule (same as every power-down):** the HPA comes down before its
drive does, and comes back up only after drive is restored. An enabled HPA with
the BUC muted amplifies raw noise into the feed - that is an equipment-damage
event, not a judgment call.
</Aside>

## BUC Over-Temperature

The BUC's thermal protection is intended to mute the output if it reaches the
over-temperature threshold. When that protection does not fire on its own, the operator
mutes manually. **Muting is the action that allows cooling to begin** - an unmuted BUC
continues to dissipate power and the temperature will not fall.

### Recovery

1. Disable the HPA output FIRST - never mute the BUC under an enabled HPA.
2. Mute the BUC.
3. Confirm temperature trending downward.
4. Wait for temperature `< 70°C` and current draw `< 4.5 A`.
5. Do not unmute until both conditions hold.

---

## LNB Reference Unlock

The GPSDO is locked and the 10 MHz reference is available upstream. The LNB itself has
a sticky reference-lock fault - a transient that the LNB's internal phase-lock circuitry
cannot recover from without a power cycle.

### Recovery

1. Power OFF the LNB.
2. Wait for state to update.
3. Power ON the LNB.
4. Confirm reference lock acquired.
5. Confirm thermal stabilization complete before relying on the link.

<Aside type="note">
Power cycling the LNB briefly drops your receive path. While you are mid-recovery on a
muted TX chain anyway, this is a natural moment to do it. If the BUC were still up, you
would coordinate the LNB cycle with the customer or wait for a low-traffic window.
</Aside>

---

## HPA Back-off Drift

Back-off measures how far below saturation the HPA is operating. The standard for
TIDEMARK-1 service is 10 dB - enough margin to keep IMD products below the coordination
floor while delivering SLA-grade power.

Below 3 dB the amplifier is overdriven: IMD products rise sharply, adjacent-channel
emissions exceed coordination limits, and the amplifier's tube wears prematurely.

### Recovery

1. Restore back-off to 10 dB.
2. Confirm the `overdriven` indicator clears.
3. Verify on the spectrum after restoration that the output is clean.

The back-off setting can be corrected while the HPA output is disabled and the BUC
muted - the value applies when the amplifier comes back up. The amplifier will be
ready when you unmute the BUC and re-enable the output.

## Documentation

Concurrent unrelated faults must be logged so the next operator inherits the full
picture. A correct entry captures:

- Time and station.
- Each fault, **separately** identified.
- The order of recovery actions and the reasoning.
- Customer impact and customer notification status.

Example:

> *"0934 - VT-01 cascade event. Concurrent unrelated faults: BUC over-temp (72°C, high I),
> LNB ref unlock (sticky), HPA back-off drift (2 dB). Recovery in order: BUC mute, HPA
> 10 dB, LNB power cycle, BUC unmute. SeaLink (Okafor) notified. Impact report to
> follow."*

Trend the BUC temperature and HPA back-off readings against prior shifts - if either
fault recurs, the next event is no longer "unrelated."
