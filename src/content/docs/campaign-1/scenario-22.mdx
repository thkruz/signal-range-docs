### Beacon Power History (monthly medians, ME/VT combined)

| Month | Relative beacon level (dB) |
|-------|----------------------------|
| 36 months ago | 0.0 (reference) |
| 30 months ago | -0.6 |
| 24 months ago | -1.3 |
| 18 months ago | -2.2 |
| 12 months ago | -3.3 |
| 9 months ago | -4.0 |
| 6 months ago | -4.7 |
| 3 months ago | -5.5 |
| This month | **-6.3** |
| *Step-track floor (beacon lock: 6.5 dB C/N on the ACU, 1 kHz tracking bandwidth)* | *about -8.6* |

The 36-month reference read about **-63 dBm** on the VT-01 spectrum analyzer's Peak
readout, so this month's median is about -69 dBm. The log is always taken the same
way: step-tracked, beacon at 1,085 MHz IF on the **2 kHz span (RBW 3 Hz)** preset,
with nothing of ours on the bird. The span matters: a wider span brings a wider RBW,
which adds noise power to a CW line's Peak reading (about -67.7 dBm at 10 kHz and
-66 dBm at 1 MHz on the same beacon), so only a 2 kHz reading compares with the log. The AGC
then levels on noise alone and the alarm list shows "AGC on noise only - passband
power is noise, no traffic carrier": the beacon is a narrow CW line, far below the
noise integrated across the whole passband, so that alarm is expected while you
measure. Keying a carrier makes the AGC back off and the beacon reads about 12 dB
lower.

Today the step-tracked beacon reads about 9 dB C/N on the ACU, so about 2.3 dB
separates it from the 6.5 dB beacon lock below which step-track cannot hold the
bird. At the last six months' rate (1.6 dB per six months) that margin lasts two to
three quarters, sooner if the slope keeps steepening: plan to move customers inside
two.

The decline is consistent with solar array / power subsystem aging: the bus is
running out of watts, and the spacecraft sheds load by reducing RF output. The
slope has been **steepening** - every six months has lost more than the six before
(0.6, 0.7, 0.9, 1.1, 1.4, 1.6 dB), and the last 6 months alone lost more than the
whole first year of the record (1.3 dB).

<Aside type="note">
Step-track needs a usable beacon. The operational cliff is not "the transponder
dies" - it is "the beacon gets too weak to track an inclined bird," at which
point every pass becomes a manual-pointing exercise and service quality falls
off fast. The recommendation hinges on when the trend crosses that line, with
margin for the months you cannot see yet.
</Aside>

## Assessment Framework (the report you are building)

| Section | Question it answers |
|---------|--------------------|
| Data | What did today's run actually measure? |
| Trend | What is the decline rate, and is it accelerating? |
| Risk | What fails first, and what does failure look like for customers? |
| Recommendation | Sunset window with migration plan and decision points |

A defensible recommendation names its assumptions: the trend continues or
steepens, no single-event failures, and tracking - not the payload - is the
binding constraint.

## Escalation Guidelines

This is an analysis shift; the escalation risk is overstating confidence.
Numbers you measured, you state. Numbers you extrapolated, you label. The board
can handle uncertainty; it cannot handle false precision that unravels in a
quarter.
