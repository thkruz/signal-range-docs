## Interference Mitigation Equipment

### Notch Filter Quick Reference

The receive chain includes a configurable notch filter for narrowband interference removal.

| Parameter | Range | Notes |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| Center Frequency | 950-2150 MHz | Set to interference IF frequency |
| Bandwidth | 100 kHz - 10 MHz | Match interference width |
| Depth | 40 dB | Set on the Notch Filter card (adjustable) |
| State | Enabled/Disabled | Filter bypassed when disabled |

<Aside type="caution">
Notch filters remove signal energy at the configured frequency. If placed incorrectly, you'll remove wanted signal instead of interference. Verify the interference frequency on the spectrum analyzer before configuring the notch.
</Aside>

### When Notching Works

Notch filters are effective against:
- Narrowband interference (CW tones, narrow carriers)
- Stable, non-hopping interference
- Interference not centered on your carrier

They cannot help with broadband interference or interference centered on your signal.

A notch in our receive chain cleans up only our own receiver. Interference inside the transponder is on the downlink every terminal in the beam receives, so SeaLink's ships keep seeing it until its source is fixed.

## C/N Degradation Quick Reference

| Margin (Es/N0 - 5.0 dB) | C/N, clean carrier | Modem Reads | Service Impact |
|-------------------------|--------------------|-------------|----------------|
| about 5.8 dB or more | about 10 dB or more | Locked, good margin | Normal operations, fade margin in hand |
| 1.0 to 5.8 dB | about 5.2 to 10 dB | Locked, good margin | Decoding cleanly, little fade margin left |
| -0.5 to 1.0 dB | about 3.7 to 5.2 dB | Locked, **Degraded margin** | Hub still decodes error-free; customers' small terminals are already dropping packets |
| below -0.5 dB | below about 3.7 dB | **No lock** | Outage |

The modem works on Es/N0, not C/N: QPSK 3/4 needs Es/N0 5.0 dB, RX Analysis shows it as Required Es/N0, and the status bar shows the margin over it. The thresholds have hysteresis: once Degraded, the modem reads Good again only from 1.5 dB of margin; once lost, it re-acquires lock only from +0.5 dB. On a clean carrier Es/N0 reads about 0.8 dB above C/N, which is where the C/N column comes from. With interference in the band the two read closer together, so judge the link by the margin in the status bar, not by a C/N rule of thumb.

The table is for the 9 m hub at VT-01. SeaLink's ships run 1-2 m terminals with 15-20 dB less G/T, so the same interference in the transponder puts them much closer to their threshold. TIDEMARK-1's carrier is concatenated-coded (Viterbi K=7 rate 3/4 inside, Reed-Solomon 223/255 outside; the Payload Data Integrity card shows both). That pair decodes error-free while Reed-Solomon can still correct every block the Viterbi decoder hands it, then fails abruptly within a fraction of a dB: the hub's **Payload Data Integrity** card can show BER around 1e-12 and 0 CRC errors while the remotes are losing packets. Judge the customer's link by the hub's margin, not its BER.

C/N drops when carrier power decreases OR when in-band noise/interference increases: an interferer inside our passband counts as noise to the demodulator (C/(N+I)), and one inside the transponder also takes a share of its output power. The receiver's AGC responds to total power in the passband, so a spike makes its gain sit lower (the AGC card shows only the current GAIN and INPUT; a narrow spike is worth well under a dB) - but that gain applies to carrier and noise alike, so the AGC's gain change is a symptom of the extra power, not the cause of the C/N loss.

## Diagnostic Checklist

### Initial Assessment
- [ ] Check receiver modem C/N display
- [ ] Compare to nominal (14+ dB expected)
- [ ] Note current degradation severity

### Spectrum Analysis
- [ ] Reconfigure analyzer: widen span to 50+ MHz, RBW Auto, amplitude -100 to -30 dBm
- [ ] Center on main signal IF (1,532 MHz)
- [ ] Look for anomalies in or near the signal bandwidth
- [ ] Characterize any interference: frequency, bandwidth, power

### Interference Identification
- [ ] Determine interference IF frequency
- [ ] Calculate RF frequency using LO (IF = LO - RF for high-side conversion)
- [ ] Assess whether narrowband or broadband
- [ ] Evaluate if notch filtering is appropriate

### Mitigation
- [ ] Configure notch filter center frequency
- [ ] Set notch bandwidth (cover interference with minimal margin)
- [ ] Enable notch filter
- [ ] Verify C/N recovery on receiver modem

### Service Verification
- [ ] Confirm C/N back above the 8 dB service minimum (expect about 9-10 dB with the notch: the spike still takes its share of transponder power, so the full 14 dB returns only when its source is fixed)
- [ ] Monitor for stability
- [ ] Update trouble ticket with resolution
