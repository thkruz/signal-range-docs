## Where Predictions Come From

Everything in the **Observations** tab — AOS, elevation, azimuth, the
countdown — is computed. None of it is measured. The chain looks like this:

```
Volunteer mirror site  →  TLE text file  →  local propagator  →  your pass list
```

A TLE is two lines of numbers describing an orbit. It has no signature, no
checksum that means anything against tampering, and no chain of custody. If
the file is wrong — stale, corrupted, or edited by somebody — your pass list
is wrong in exactly the same way, confidently, with no error message.

<Aside type="caution">
A prediction and an observation are different kinds of statement. The
Observations tab tells you what the *math* expects. The waterfall tells you
what the *sky* is doing. When those two disagree, one of them is wrong, and it
is your job to find out which — not to pick the one you like.
</Aside>

The **Observations** panel can fetch a fresh element set from the network if
you have reason to distrust what's loaded.

## End of Briefing

The network is trusting a stranger's backyard with a real observation request.
You're trusting a stranger's website with the numbers that tell you where to
point.

Both of those are fine. They're how the hobby works, and how a lot of serious
science works too. But trust that can't be cross-checked isn't trust — it's
just a habit.

Two sources. One sky. If they don't match, find out why.
