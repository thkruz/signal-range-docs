## Tonight's Pass

| Satellite | AOS | Max Elevation | LOS | What It Sends |
|-----------|-----|---------------|-----|---------------|
| WXSAT-19 | 16:03 (T+3 min) | 55° at 16:10 | 16:18 | APT weather imagery, continuous |

Fifty-five degrees is a good pass — better than most. WXSAT-19 is a polar
orbiter: it comes up in the northwest, crosses high overhead, and sets in the
southeast about fifteen minutes later. While it's up it draws a picture of the
weather below it, one scan line at a time, and anyone with an antenna can
listen.

The **Observations** tab has the live countdown. Check it before you go
hunting on the waterfall.

</Aside>

## The Rig

| Component | What It Is | Notes |
|-----------|-----------|-------|
| Antenna | Hand-wound quadrifilar helix, 137 MHz | Zip-tied to the fence, pointed straight up, 3 dBi |
| Receiver | RTL-SDR v3, direct sampling | RF goes straight into the ADC — no downconversion |
| Software | SkyWatcher SDR Console | Waterfall, VFO, decoder |

The QFH's beam is roughly **140° wide**. That is the entire trick: a big dish
has to be aimed and followed, but three decibels of gain buys a beam so fat
that the satellite spends the whole pass inside it. Big antennas point. Little
antennas wait.

## Link Reference

Direct sampling means there's no LO and no IF math. What you tune is what's on
the air.

| Signal | Frequency | Bandwidth | Mode |
|--------|-----------|-----------|------|
| WXSAT-19 APT downlink | 137.100 MHz | 34 kHz | BPSK 1/2 |

### Doppler

At 137 MHz, a low-earth pass shifts the carrier by roughly **±3 kHz** across
the pass. That's real, and you can watch the stripe lean on the waterfall —
but it fits inside a properly-set channel, so once you're locked you do not
need to chase it.

Remember that number. It will not be true on 70cm, and Scenario 2 is going to
make you feel it.

<Aside type="caution">
A receiver channel narrower than the signal doesn't give you a weak, noisy
copy — it gives you **nothing**. The demodulator never sees a signal it can't
fit. If the stripe is plainly on the waterfall and the radio insists there's
no lock, stop tuning and look at the channel width.
</Aside>

## Getting a Picture

Three things have to be true at once, and the console will tell you which one
you're missing:

- [ ] **On frequency** — VFO within a few kHz of the APT downlink. Click the
      stripe on the waterfall, use the bookmark, or walk it over with the tune
      buttons.
- [ ] **Channel open** — wide enough to bracket 34 kHz of signal. Too narrow
      and it's invisible; far too wide and you're just collecting noise.
- [ ] **Left alone** — once the decoder is running, hands off. The picture
      builds one line at a time and every fiddle costs you scan lines.

The lock indicator diagnoses itself. `NO LOCK` means nothing's there.
`BW TOO NARROW` means the passband is lifted but the channel can't fit what's
in it. `LOCKED` means stop touching things.
