## CUBEHOP-1 Transponder Plan

CUBEHOP-1 carries a **linear transponder**: it listens on one frequency,
amplifies whatever it hears, and retransmits it on another. Everything in the
passband goes up and comes back down. That is the entire design.

| Path | Frequency | Bandwidth | Polarization |
|------|-----------|-----------|--------------|
| Uplink (you transmit) | 435.900 MHz | 30 kHz | RHCP |
| Downlink (you receive) | 435.290 MHz | 30 kHz | RHCP |
| FM beacon (unrelated) | 435.250 MHz | 15 kHz | RHCP |

<Aside type="caution">
The transponder passband is 30 kHz wide. Tune the uplink sloppily and you fall
off the edge of it — the bird simply doesn't hear you, and nothing on your
console will explain why.
</Aside>

### The Doppler Asymmetry

Your uplink leaves a transmitter bolted to Vermont, so it does not Doppler.
The **downlink** does, because it comes from a satellite doing 7.5 km/s. You
will hear your own signal walk across the waterfall while the thing producing
it sits perfectly still on your desk.

AFC handles it. Let it.

## Band Plan Notes (exam material)

70cm is **shared**. It is allocated by agreement and coordinated by people who
have never met you, and essentially nothing in your radio enforces any of it.

What makes a transmission legal is the overlap of three separate things:

1. The frequency is in a sub-band your license covers.
2. You hold that license, and you are the control operator responsible for
   everything that leaves this yard.
3. The satellite in question is open for amateur use on that frequency.

And one rule that surprises everybody: **no encryption**. Not because it's
technically hard — because amateur radio polices itself. Every operator has to
be able to identify every transmission and who sent it. Openness *is* the
enforcement mechanism.

Hold that thought for the 15:55 pass.

Somewhere between the exam and the evening pass you are going to learn the
thing this whole campaign has been circling: the radio spectrum does not
authenticate anybody. Not the pirate, not the impostor, and not you. A license

geometry, and the clock.

Everything else is just a signal claiming to be something.
