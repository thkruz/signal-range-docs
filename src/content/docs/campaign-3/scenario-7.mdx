## The Interference

| Property | Observed |
|----------|----------|
| Center frequency | ~435.360 MHz |
| Width | ~80 kHz of ragged hash |
| Behavior | Continuous — no on/off pattern |
| Doppler | None |
| In the Observations list | No |

Three of those lines are the diagnosis. Satellites drift, rise, and set;
ground noise sits perfectly still and changes strength with **where your beam
is pointing**, not with time.

Note also that it is *continuous*. Deliberate interference usually has a duty
cycle — somebody keying up, something scanning. Accidents just run until
somebody unplugs them.

## Direction Finding with a Yagi

The crossed yagi has **12 dBi** forward gain, a **40°** beam, and — this is
the part that matters tonight — an **18 dB front-to-back ratio**.

That means the same antenna reads a ground signal dramatically louder pointed
at it than pointed away from it. Sweep the beam around the horizon with the
**MAN AZ** control in the ROTATOR panel, watch the hash strength, and the peak
is your bearing. That's a foxhunt, and it's the same instrument you'd use on a
signal claiming to be in orbit.

---

## Finding the Decibels

Three separate places, and you need all three tonight:

- [ ] **Bearing** — sweep the yagi, find where the hash peaks, know your enemy.
- [ ] **Notch** — the FILTER section has a one-knob notch. Center it on the
      hash and it drops out of your passband. You can't unplug the neighbor's
      gear; you can decline to listen to it.
- [ ] **IF filter width** — the front end is wide open at 200 kHz. The noise
      gate charges you by the hertz: narrow the IF filter to **100 kHz** and
      the floor comes down with it.

Then fly the pass: program-track on CUBEHOP-1, and hold the lock through
eighteen degrees. AFC is allowed tonight — you proved you could do it by hand
three sessions ago.
