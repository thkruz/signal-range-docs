## Note on the Pass Card

> The satellite is only above us for a few minutes. Connect both paths and find
> its downlink before we transmit anything.
>
> Listen first. If you can't hear yourself coming back, stop transmitting.
>
> — Riley

## Kit

| Item | Class | Notes |
|------|-------|-------|
| Antenna | Directional dual-band antenna | Pointed by hand |
| Transmit radio | 5 W dual-band FM handheld | UHF uplink |
| Receive path | USB SDR and laptop | VHF downlink, with isolation from the transmitter |
| Audio | Headphones | Avoid speaker feedback |
| Power | Battery | |
| Data | Pass information | Current authored orbital elements |

A two-radio arrangement is an acceptable alternative. Two devices on one
antenna do not by themselves give full-duplex operation; the transmitter can
desensitize the receiver unless isolation is provided.

<Aside type="note">
Hearing your own voice back through the satellite is not a two-person contact.
A weaker signal does not always mean a broken cable; check pointing,
polarization and frequency drift first.
</Aside>

---

## Pass Checklist

- [ ] Pass card read: rise direction, usable interval, bands, mode
- [ ] Both radio paths connected with isolation, headphones on
- [ ] Antenna pointed until the downlink is audible, receive only at first
- [ ] Downlink retuned through the pass for Doppler, manual before automatic
- [ ] Uplink power and tone set modestly while monitoring the downlink
- [ ] Call sign and grid locator exchanged with another station; time left for others
- [ ] Fading causes told apart: rotation, polarization, frequency drift, obstruction

---

## End of Briefing

The window is short and the pass will not wait. Be ready on the downlink before
you press the transmit button.
